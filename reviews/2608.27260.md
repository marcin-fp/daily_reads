## What this paper is about

Training an LLM agent to use tools, browse the web, or edit code requires interaction data that's much richer than an instruction-response pair: a consistent environment, a task that's actually achievable in it, a realized interaction, and (often) a way to check success. This survey argues the agentic-data-generation literature is fragmented by domain labels (tool-use papers, coding papers, GUI papers) in a way that obscures shared generation mechanisms, and proposes two organizing devices to fix that: a common four-part factorization of any piece of agentic data, and a lens called ACE (Accuracy–Complexity–divErsity) for judging whether a generation pipeline's output distribution is actually useful, independent of domain.

## Extended summary

**The common data object.** Any piece of agentic data is factored as d = (E, q, τ, v): an environment specification E (tools, state, transition rules, policies, observation interface), a task signal q (what should be achieved, grounded relative to E), an interaction realization τ (a demonstrated or sampled trajectory), and an optional verifier v (schema checker, executable test, terminal-state predicate, LLM judge, or a hybrid). The same four roles instantiate across tool use, software engineering, web/GUI, embodied, social/multi-agent, and scientific/formal domains, differing only in concrete representation (Figure 2).

**Generation paradigms.** Pipelines are organized by which factor anchors generation and in what dependency order, not by domain. Forward generation follows E→q→τ: build an environment (from real APIs/repos, LLM-synthesized tools, or programmatic/executable construction), generate grounded tasks, then realize interactions — offering strong grounding but cascading dependence on environment quality. Reverse generation changes the anchor: task-first (specify a target capability/goal, then build environment and trajectory around it), trajectory-first (explore or mine an existing environment/workflow, then derive the task from what was found), and structure-first (generate an intermediate plan/graph/blueprint that coordinates the other factors). A cross-cutting fifth category, adaptive/self-evolving generation, continually revises generation strategy using accumulated experience, verification outcomes, and observed coverage gaps rather than producing independent samples from a fixed generator.

**The ACE objective.** Accuracy is framed as a non-compensable admission gate, not a benefit tradeable against other properties: a conjunctive validity condition requiring the environment to be internally consistent, the task feasible and grounded, the interaction causally valid under the environment and task, and the verifier aligned with the intended outcome — a correct-looking trajectory does not compensate for an infeasible task, and vice versa. Within that accuracy-filtered support, Complexity is defined as model- and configuration-relative difficulty (a verified failure probability under a declared learner, tools, verifier, and budget) rather than an intrinsic property like length or tool count; useful data sits in a moving "learnable band" near the current learner's capability frontier, not at either extreme. Diversity is a batch-level property rewarding balanced, non-redundant coverage across environment/task/interaction factors after discounting behaviorally (not just textually) redundant samples — renamed tools or paraphrased requests that induce identical behavior do not count as diversity.

**Synthesis by dimension.** For Accuracy (Section 4), the survey traces a shift from layered rule/model/human checking, through constraint-grounded construction (verifying blueprints or graphs before realization), toward execution- and state-based verification (grounding checks in real tool/environment execution rather than plausibility judgment) and feedback-based repair (regenerating failed components rather than only filtering them out). It flags that verifiers can only assess what their rules/criteria represent, that repeated optimization against a fixed verifier can produce verifier-friendly rather than genuinely correct solutions, and that accuracy should be reported per factor and failure category rather than as one aggregate pass rate. For Complexity (Section 5), it separates structural proxies (horizon, tool count, branching) from actual model-relative difficulty, and surveys mechanisms for calibrating difficulty to a moving learner frontier (failure-driven calibration, bidirectional scaffolding for too-hard cases). For Diversity (Section 6), it proposes coverage and normalized-entropy statistics per factor, warns against "template locking" (marginally diverse factors that only ever co-occur in fixed combinations), and argues diversity claims should be paired with validity rates and transfer evidence on held-out factors, not asserted from sample count alone.

**Discussion (Section 7).** Under ACE, data scaling should be measured as growth in accurate, learnable, non-redundant "effective support" rather than raw example count — the survey cites specific results where diversity-oriented scaling outperformed quantity scaling at equal or smaller budget, and where a smaller set of robustly verified environments outperformed a larger, less reliable collection. Real and synthetic data are argued to be complementary rather than substitutable across all three ACE dimensions (real data anchors authenticity and enables external audits; synthetic data provides controlled coverage and explicit supervision), favoring hybrid allocation over a fixed mixing ratio. The survey extends ACE to agentic pre-/mid-training (where a training unit need not be a complete (E,q,τ,v) record, and accuracy shifts toward local transition correctness rather than full task verification) and to self-evolving agents (where ACE becomes dynamic, and the survey warns that a generator, learner, and verifier that all adapt together can jointly confirm the same mistaken assumptions unless anchored by fixed or independently updated held-out evaluation).

## Learnings

The single most transferable idea is the asymmetric structure of ACE itself: treating validity as a non-compensable gate rather than one input among several to be traded off against difficulty or novelty is a clean, general principle for any data- or experience-curation pipeline, not just agentic post-training — it directly formalizes the intuition that a harder or more novel example is worthless if it isn't actually valid, and prevents "impressive-looking but broken" data from being admitted just because it scores well on other axes.

The "template locking" observation (marginal diversity across separate factors can coexist with near-zero joint diversity, when factors that appear varied on their own only ever occur in fixed combinations) is a specific, actionable measurement pitfall: reporting per-factor coverage or entropy alone can create an illusion of diversity that a joint-distribution check (tool family × task capability, state regime × required outcome) would immediately reveal.

The survey's treatment of complexity as strictly learner- and configuration-relative (the same instance can be trivial for one model/tool-access/budget combination and productively hard for another) is a reusable corrective against static "difficulty" labels attached to a dataset independent of who trains on it — a dataset's difficulty claim is only meaningful relative to a declared execution configuration.

## Verification

Not applicable in the usual empirical sense: this survey conducts no original experiments and reports no new measurements. Its evidentiary basis is the literature it organizes: mechanism-level claims are attributed to specific cited pipelines (e.g., attributing "a smaller set of robustly verified environments outperforms a larger unreliable one" to a specific environment-scaling study), and the survey does not independently re-verify any of these underlying results. The three ACE dimensions are each accompanied by an explicit "tradeoffs and limitations" subsection (4.4, 5.5, 6.5) that surfaces known failure modes (verifier bias, accuracy-diversity tension, mixture interference) rather than presenting the framework uncritically, which is a meaningful form of self-scrutiny for a framework-proposing survey even without original verification.

## Field context

This survey sits at the intersection of agentic post-training and data-centric AI, explicitly positioning itself against domain-siloed prior reviews (tool-use-specific, coding-specific, GUI-specific literatures) by proposing mechanism-level rather than domain-level organization. Within this same batch, its Accuracy-as-non-compensable-gate framing is a strikingly direct conceptual match to BCIT's design in "Knowing When Not to Reuse" ([2608.26730.md](2608.26730.md)): BCIT's multiplicative combination of source-evidence strength and current-context compatibility, with a separate non-compensable hard-conflict veto, is structurally the same idea this survey states abstractly for accuracy versus complexity/diversity — strong evidence on one axis (historical success, or high difficulty/novelty) cannot buy past a failure on the gating axis (context compatibility, or basic validity). Neither paper cites the other, so this is a genuine independent convergence on the same design principle from two different problem framings (data generation versus experience-reuse authorization) rather than a shared lineage. The survey's Section 7.4 on self-evolving agents and the risk of a generator/learner/verifier "confirming the same mistaken assumptions" together is also a direct, more abstract restatement of the concrete problem BCIT's evaluated system is built to solve.

## Critical discussion

As an organizing survey rather than an empirical study, the relevant critical questions are whether the proposed taxonomy and lens are well-grounded in the literature they organize, and whether the formal apparatus is presented with appropriate humility about what it actually establishes. On the first count, the survey does a genuinely careful job: the four-factor data object and the forward/reverse generation taxonomy map cleanly onto a large, heterogeneous set of cited pipelines (over 100 distinct methods across six domains) without visibly forcing awkward classifications, and each ACE dimension's "tradeoffs and limitations" subsection actively surfaces failure modes rather than presenting the framework as a solved problem.

One point is worth flagging directly: the ACE objective is repeatedly presented in formal mathematical notation — a conjunctive validity predicate for Accuracy, a verified-failure-probability definition for Complexity, an entropy-and-redundancy-penalized coverage functional for Diversity — that reads like a specified, potentially implementable training objective. In fact, none of these formal objects is instantiated, fit, or evaluated anywhere in the paper; they function purely as descriptive scaffolding for organizing existing pipelines' design choices in a common notation, and the paper itself acknowledges this explicitly ("a design principle rather than a universal score... its factor representation, entropy estimator, redundancy measure, weights... should be specified for each domain"). This self-disclosure mitigates but does not eliminate the risk that a reader skimming the equations could mistake ACE for a working, benchmarked method rather than a conceptual lens (`overclaim`, in the narrow sense of formal presentation outrunning what has actually been operationalized or tested). Separately, the survey's synthesis claims about field-level trends (a "shift toward execution-grounded accuracy, learner-relative complexity, and diversity beyond surface variation") are supported by citing individual papers that each made a narrower, specific claim; the survey's own framing imposes a directional narrative across these independent results that is plausible and well-cited but is itself an interpretive synthesis rather than a claim independently measured across the field as a whole.

What remains valuable after these observations: the common factorization and forward/reverse taxonomy give the field a genuinely useful shared vocabulary that the survey demonstrates works across six very different domains, and the ACE lens's central asymmetric-gate principle (validity is not tradeable against difficulty or novelty) is a sound, well-motivated design rule regardless of whether its formal notation is ever operationalized as a literal scoring function.
