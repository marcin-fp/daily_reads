Citation: Huben, R., Morris, V. Attention-Only Transformers and Implementing MLPs with Attention Heads. arXiv:2309.08593, 2023.

## What this paper is about

A standard transformer layer has two very different-looking parts: self-attention, which lets tokens exchange information dynamically based on content, and an MLP, which processes each token's representation independently through a fixed, static transformation. Mechanistic interpretability researchers have found attention-only transformers easier to reverse-engineer than ones with MLP layers, so this paper asks whether that asymmetry is actually load-bearing: could an MLP always be rewritten as attention instead? The authors prove mathematically that it can, for a broad class of activation functions, by constructing an equivalent stack of attention heads (each of internal dimension 1) that reproduces exactly what an MLP layer computes, meaning any standard transformer can in principle be rewritten as a pure attention-only transformer, at the cost of an enormous increase in the number of attention heads required.

## Extended summary

The central object is a "generalized SiLU" activation function, a family that includes SiLU itself and close numerical approximations of GeLU and ReLU (the paper cites specific published approximation-error bounds for both). The paper's key intuitive observation, stated plainly before the formal proof, is that softmax applied to a two-way choice ([-x, 0]) produces exactly [sigma(x), sigma(-x)], the sigmoid gate at the heart of the SiLU activation, so a masked attention pattern can be engineered to reproduce that gate exactly. Theorem 3 formalizes this: given an MLP with no bias terms, one hidden layer of size l, and a generalized-SiLU activation, there exist l separate masked attention heads, each with internal dimension exactly 1, whose sum exactly reproduces the MLP's output, provided the residual stream is augmented with an extra "bias token" dimension (explicitly compared by the authors to the persistent memory vectors of Sukhbaatar et al. 2019's all-attention architecture) and a nonstandard masking pattern in which each token attends only to itself and this single static bias token (rather than the usual causal/local attention pattern). The proof is constructive: it explicitly derives the parameter and mask matrices for each attention head and verifies algebraically that the masked-softmax computation reduces term-by-term to the MLP's own hidden-unit computation.

Theorem 6 extends this from a single layer to an entire transformer: if every MLP layer in a network uses a generalized-SiLU activation, the whole network can be converted, layer by layer via induction, into an architecture using only attention heads, with the residual stream carrying one extra dimension throughout to hold the bias-token value. The proof handles both cases in the induction, converting an original MLP sublayer using Theorem 3, and re-deriving equivalent (dimension-adjusted) attention heads for sublayers that were already attention in the original network, so that the augmented residual stream stays consistent layer to layer. The authors note this construction depends critically on skip connections: without them, the bias-token value the construction relies on gets zeroed out at the very next layer, and they connect this observation to a separate negative result (Dong et al. 2021) showing that pure self-attention networks without skip connections or MLPs collapse rank-1 with depth.

Section 4 shows the two components of an MLP, the row-wise linear transformation and the entrywise nonlinearity, can also be implemented by attention heads separately rather than only as a single fused unit: Theorem 7 shows an attention head with an identity mask matrix (each token attends only to itself) reduces to a pure linear transformation of that token's own representation, and Theorem 8 applies the same construction as Theorem 3 to derive attention heads that apply a generalized-SiLU nonlinearity entrywise (via D attention heads for a D-dimensional residual stream), which combined with a skip connection reproduces the nonlinearity's effect on the residual stream.

Section 5 addresses an aesthetic objection to the earlier constructions, that their masking patterns are artificially restrictive (attend only to self and one bias token) rather than the standard causal or other masking patterns actually used in practice. Theorem 9 proves that any target masking pattern can be approximated arbitrarily closely by instead encoding the mask directly into the attention head's own weight matrix (via an added, sufficiently large scaling term Omega multiplying an identity-augmented residual stream), such that the approximation error shrinks toward zero as Omega grows, with the required rate of growth shown to depend only logarithmically on the desired error tolerance. Example 11 works this out numerically for GPT-2, plugging in published or informally-reported bounds on GPT-2's weight-matrix norms and residual-stream magnitude (drawn from a peer-reviewed architecture paper for the model's dimensions, but from two non-peer-reviewed AlignmentForum blog posts for the empirical weight-distribution and residual-stream-norm bounds) to derive a concrete sufficient value of the scaling parameter Omega for machine-precision-level accuracy.

The Limitations section (Section 6), written candidly by the authors themselves, identifies three specific practical obstacles to the construction: the attention-head count required is enormous (one head per hidden-layer dimension, illustrated concretely with GPT-3's would-be 49,152 additional single-dimension attention heads per layer); the reformulation likely increases computational cost since it replaces efficient matrix multiplication with many separate vector-level operations, many of whose outputs are simply discarded by masking; and the pseudo-masking technique from Theorem 9 introduces its own training difficulties, since the large Omega terms it requires would interact poorly with standard regularization techniques like dropout and L2 weight penalties. The Discussion closes by explicitly naming the paper's practical goal (extending Elhage et al. 2021's attention-only-transformer interpretability techniques to MLP layers, which that prior work found comparatively hard to interpret) and just as explicitly naming the obstacle to achieving it: MLP layers in a model like GPT-3 already outnumber attention layers 2:1 by parameter count, but the paper's own construction would make that gap 500:1 by attention-head count, leading the authors to offer, somewhat wryly, that "scale is all you need" may apply as much to the difficulty of mechanistic interpretability as it does to capabilities.

## Learnings

The paper's central transferable idea is a concrete, constructive equivalence between two mechanisms that are usually treated as categorically different parts of a transformer: a masked attention head with internal dimension 1, attending only to a token itself and one static reference point, is mathematically exactly an MLP neuron with a SiLU-family activation. This reframes "what is an MLP neuron" as a special, extremely restricted case of "what is an attention head" rather than a separate primitive, which is a specific, checkable answer (not just an analogy) to a question, whether the field's two main architectural components are as distinct as they appear, that is easy to leave at the level of intuition.

A second, more cautionary learning comes from the paper's own honest reckoning with what the construction actually buys: converting MLPs to attention heads to make them more interpretable (by reusing techniques that work well on attention-only networks) is mathematically valid but practically self-defeating at scale, since the conversion inflates the attention-head count far more than it inflates parameter count, potentially making the resulting network harder rather than easier to mechanistically analyze. This is a specific instance of a more general lesson worth carrying elsewhere: a theoretically valid reformulation into a "more interpretable" primitive is not automatically a practical interpretability win if the reformulation's own scale cost outpaces the interpretability gain per unit.

A third, more technical learning is the specific mechanism the paper uses to bridge an artificial, restricted masking pattern (attend only to self and a bias token) to an arbitrary target masking pattern: rather than changing the network's structure, Theorem 9 shows the mask can be encoded directly into the attention weight matrix itself via a single large scaling parameter, with the approximation error controlled by how large that parameter is made, at a rate that only grows logarithmically in the desired precision. This "encode a discrete structural choice into a continuous weight via a scaling limit" technique is a reusable proof pattern for showing one architectural variant can approximate another to arbitrary precision.

## Verification

As a pure theory paper making no empirical claims, this paper's verification standard is mathematical proof rather than experiment, and its proofs are checked at a level of rigor appropriate to that standard: Theorem 3's construction is proven first in a restricted special case (a single hidden unit, unit-scaled activation) via direct algebraic substitution, then generalized in two separate, explicit steps (to arbitrary scaling constants, then to arbitrary hidden-layer size via summation over independently-constructed heads), which is a disciplined way to verify a general claim by building it up from a fully worked base case rather than asserting the general form directly. Theorem 6's induction is checked in both branches of its case split (the original sublayer being an MLP, or already being attention), rather than only the more interesting MLP case, closing a gap that would otherwise leave the "convert the whole network" claim only partially justified. Theorem 9's approximation guarantee is proven with an explicit epsilon-delta-style convergence argument (a genuine, worked bound on the scaling parameter needed for any target error, not just an asymptotic "as Omega goes to infinity" claim), and the authors go further by computing an explicit numerical value for a real model (Example 11, GPT-2), a concrete instantiation that lets a reader sanity-check the abstract bound against realistic architectural numbers rather than leaving it purely symbolic. The one place this otherwise careful verification standard weakens is exactly that numerical example: two of its three input quantities (the empirical bound on weight-matrix distributions, and the empirical bound on residual-stream norm growth) are sourced from informal, non-peer-reviewed AlignmentForum blog posts rather than a peer-reviewed measurement, so the specific numerical answer computed for GPT-2 inherits whatever uncertainty those informal sources carry, uncertainty the paper does not itself quantify or caveat when presenting the final number.

## Field context

This paper sits squarely in the mechanistic-interpretability line of work it names directly as its motivation, Elhage et al.'s (2021) "A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits," which found attention-only transformers substantially more tractable to reverse-engineer than ones including MLP layers and explicitly flagged MLP interpretability as unfinished business; this paper's entire contribution is a proposed bridge for reusing attention-only-transformer interpretability tooling on MLP layers by first proving the two are interchangeable. It also draws a direct technical line to Sukhbaatar et al.'s (2019) persistent-memory "all-attention" architecture (the paper's bias-token construction is explicitly presented as a special case of that architecture's persistent vectors) and engages a genuine negative result, Dong et al.'s (2021) finding that pure self-attention without skip connections or MLPs collapses to rank-1 with depth, using it to explain precisely why the paper's own construction depends on skip connections rather than treating that dependency as incidental. Within this corpus, the paper stands in a striking mirror relationship to [2105.08050] (gMLP, "Pay Attention to MLPs," reviewed in a previous batch): that paper empirically showed self-attention is not necessary for strong performance because a gated MLP-like spatial mechanism can substitute for it, while this paper proves the mathematical converse, that MLPs are not a distinct primitive because attention heads (with a restricted masking pattern and dimension) can exactly reproduce them. Read together, the two papers establish from opposite directions (one empirical and performance-focused, one theoretical and equivalence-focused) that the field's two canonical transformer sublayer types are far more mutually substitutable than their standard, alternating-block presentation suggests, though gMLP's substitution is a practical, trained architecture while this paper's is an existence proof with an extreme, currently impractical scale cost. The paper's framing of an MLP neuron as "an attention head with internal dimension 1 and a particularly restrictive masking pattern" is also a natural formal complement to [2012.14913]'s (Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories, also reviewed previously) empirical characterization of MLP neurons as key-value memory lookups: both papers, independently and via different methods (one a hand-annotated empirical study, one a closed-form mathematical proof), converge on treating an MLP neuron as fundamentally an associative-retrieval operation rather than an opaque nonlinearity, though neither paper cites the other.

## Critical discussion

The paper's mathematical claims are, as far as this review can verify from the surrounding prose and proof structure (the underlying display equations themselves being largely lost in extraction), soundly constructed: each theorem is proven with an explicit, checkable derivation rather than asserted, the induction in Theorem 6 is handled completely rather than leaving a case unaddressed, and the paper is unusually candid about its own limitations, naming the attention-head blow-up, the likely inference/training slowdown, and the pseudo-masking technique's incompatibility with standard regularization as concrete, specific obstacles rather than vague caveats. This degree of self-directed critique, especially the closing observation that the construction's own scale cost may undermine its stated interpretability motivation, is a genuine strength that leaves comparatively little room for external critique of the paper's central claims.

The clearest available critique is the reliance, in the paper's one concrete numerical illustration (Example 11), on empirical bounds sourced from informal, non-peer-reviewed blog posts (an AlignmentForum post on GPT-2's residual-stream norm growth, and another on the empirical distribution of language-model weights) presented alongside genuinely peer-reviewed architectural facts (GPT-2's published dimensions) without distinguishing the two evidentiary tiers; the specific numerical value of Omega computed for GPT-2 is only as reliable as those two informal inputs, and a reader taking that number at face value should be aware it rests partly on unrefereed community measurements rather than a fully peer-reviewed empirical characterization of GPT-2's internals. A second, more conceptual point, one the authors' own Discussion surfaces rather than one this review is raising independently, is that the paper's stated motivating goal, making MLP layers more amenable to attention-only interpretability techniques, is left entirely untested: no experiment in the paper checks whether an attention-only-converted network is in fact any easier to interpret in practice, and the authors' own closing remark (that the conversion's 500:1 attention-head-count blow-up might make interpretability harder rather than easier) suggests the central practical premise motivating the whole project may not hold, an open question the paper explicitly defers to future work rather than one it resolves.

What remains well supported: the mathematical equivalence between a restricted class of MLP neurons and single-dimension masked attention heads is rigorously proven and holds for a genuinely broad, named class of activation functions (SiLU and close approximations of GeLU and ReLU) rather than only a toy special case; the equivalence extends compositionally to convert an entire transformer, linear transformations and nonlinearities can be separated and each independently implemented by attention heads; and an arbitrary masking pattern can be encoded into attention weights to within any desired precision via a single scaling parameter, with an explicit, worked convergence rate rather than only an asymptotic claim.
