Citation: Bobo Li, Hao Fei, Tianjie Ju, Mong-Li Lee, and Wynne Hsu. OmniScientist: An Omni-Modal Omni-Discipline AI Scientist. arXiv preprint, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13558v1

## What this paper is about

Most "AI Scientist" agents that autonomously run a research pipeline (idea → experiment → paper) actually work from text, code, or precomputed feature tables prepared in advance by a human — they never look at the raw microscope image, seismogram, or CAD mesh itself. The authors argue this makes such systems workflow-complete but evidence-incomplete: whatever spatial, temporal, or cross-channel structure a caption or feature vector discarded is simply unavailable to the agent. OmniScientist adds a perception layer that lets the agent inspect raw multimodal evidence directly at every stage (forming the question, designing the experiment, and grounding the final claims), wrapped in a pipeline with code-enforced checks against fabrication, statistical malpractice, and post-hoc rationalization (HARKing). They demonstrate it end-to-end on 36 real datasets across 5 discipline families and 12 modalities.

## Extended summary

**Architecture.** A perception layer classifies raw evidence into 4 discipline-independent families (perceptual, symbolic, quantitative-statistical, procedural) and exposes both native numeric readers (e.g., FFT peaks) and visual renderers per modality, under a budget that discourages reflexive image rendering. Three ReAct-loop agents run in sequence — ideation (forms a falsifiable hypothesis after literature search via OpenAlex/Crossref), experiment (writes and debugs code, runs at least 4 analyses including baselines/ablations/mechanism probes), and writeup (drafts a manuscript using one of 5 discipline-specific structural templates) — with a deterministic outer pipeline that only admits a stage's output after a code-enforced predicate check, and can send a failed stage back to ideation (bounded to 2 fallbacks).

**Checks.** The idea check requires ≥5 self-screened candidate ideas, ≥3 literature searches, a stated falsification criterion, an effective-sample-size estimate, and a leakage audit, and automatically tempers absolute-novelty language ("first," "never explored") into a hedged form. The rigour check (Algorithm 1) rejects a result unless every reported number traces to real `run_python` stdout, the correction for multiple comparisons covers every test attempted (including ones later dropped, closing a common p-hacking loophole), the headline finding is drawn from a statistically supported analysis (not merely the most flattering one), and non-headline non-significant analyses are demoted to the trace rather than silently omitted. The claim check re-verifies every number in the drafted manuscript against the grounded experiment record and reverts any prose-polish edit that alters a number, citation, claim, or model name.

**Evaluation setup.** Claude Sonnet 5 is the primary reasoning backbone (also tested: GPT-5.6, GLM-5.2, Kimi K2.7, Qwen3.5 at 9B/27B/122B, Gemma-4 at 26B/31B); the perception model is pinned to Sonnet 5 in every run so perception quality never confounds a backbone comparison. Two judges from model families outside the systems under test (deepseek-v4-flash, gemini-2.5-flash-lite) score each generated paper on 7 dimensions (5 standard peer-review criteria plus multimodal grounding and factual accuracy).

**Main results.** All 36 cases complete end-to-end with the Sonnet backbone (mean composite score 6.3/10); GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.7 score similarly on their evaluated subsets (6.7, 6.5) but complete fewer cases (17/18, 6/9), while small open-weight backbones (Qwen3.5-9B, Gemma-4-26B) both complete substantially fewer cases and score markedly lower (4.0-4.3). Factual accuracy is the highest and most backbone-invariant dimension across all runs (attributed to the provenance check holding every backbone to the same standard regardless of fluency), while novelty/significance/multimodal-grounding vary more with backbone strength.

**Perception ablation.** On 5 paired cases run with a "blind" variant that receives only precomputed scalar features instead of raw data, the perception-enabled system wins 85% of head-to-head judgments, with the largest gains in multimodal grounding (+2.8) and significance (+1.8) and no gain in factual accuracy (both conditions share the same provenance check). A qualitative analysis (Table 7) shows the two conditions pursue entirely different research questions on the same task and data — e.g., the perceiving system asks whether a pathology class is a compositional mixture of tissue types by reading tile texture, while the blind system can only ask whether a confusion matrix follows a pre-supplied similarity ranking.

**Component ablation and transparency statistics.** A leave-one-out ablation (on one case) shows removing prior-art search causes the steepest quality drop (6.9→5.7) and removing the novelty check costs a full point of novelty score specifically. Across the 36 primary runs, the code-enforced checks fired 115 rejections total; the dominant single cause (51 of 115, in 26 of 36 runs) was the agent trying to present a non-significant analysis as a finding, which the demotion check caught and relegated to the trace — while outright provenance/fabrication rejections occurred exactly once across all 36 runs. Two worked case studies (a seismic noise-label audit finding 21.7% of "noise" traces actually carry coherent transient bursts; a pediatric chest-radiograph texture signature) walk through how a visual observation converts into a falsifiable, statistically validated finding with documented robustness checks (bootstrap CIs, ablations of the detection mechanism, multiple null models).

## Learnings

Enforcing statistical hygiene as executable code predicates rather than as prose instructions to the model is a concrete, demonstrated mechanism against a specific failure mode this paper documents directly: the check-rejection statistics show the dominant thing an autonomous research agent tries to get away with is not fabricating numbers (1 rejection in 36 runs) but selectively promoting a non-significant result to headline status (51 rejections in 26 runs) — a finding about where these systems actually fail that is more specific and evidence-grounded than a generic "LLMs can hallucinate" caveat.

Separating the reasoning backbone from the perception model in the experimental design (pinning perception to one fixed model across every backbone comparison) is a clean way to isolate which capability is actually responsible for an observed quality difference — a reusable control for any multi-model-role agent evaluation.

Requiring the multiple-comparison correction to count every test attempted during the debugging loop, not just the tests reported in the final draft, closes a specific and easy-to-miss p-hacking loophole (shrinking the correction denominator by simply not mentioning abandoned analyses) that a naive "report your p-value correction" instruction would not catch.

## Verification

Central and code-enforced at three separate gates (idea, rigour, claim), with the checks' own hit rates transparently reported (Table 15): 115 total rejections across 36 runs, dominated by result-selection issues rather than fabrication. The judge panel is validated for internal consistency (inter-judge Krippendorff α=0.66, near-zero self-preference bias, low score-length correlation ρ=0.16) and deliberately drawn from model families outside the systems under test to reduce same-family favoritism. What is not verified anywhere in the paper is whether the judges' scores track ground-truth scientific quality or correctness: unlike some other AI-Scientist evaluations in this corpus, no human expert study checks whether the LLM judges' novelty, soundness, or significance scores agree with human researchers' assessments of the same papers, so the entire evaluation — including the central "perception improves quality" claim — rests on LLM-judged scores whose absolute calibration against human judgment is untested.

## Field context

This paper extends the "autonomous end-to-end research agent" line (The AI Scientist, Agent Laboratory, InternAgent, Kosmos, Gottweis et al.'s Co-Scientist) along an axis those systems mostly leave unaddressed: direct perception of raw scientific evidence rather than text/code/precomputed-summary interfaces. Its own Related Work is explicit that prior scientific-agent perception use is local and fragmented (inspecting a self-generated figure, scoring a plot, reading an instrument display) rather than lifecycle-wide, and that existing multimodal-science benchmarks fix the observation and question in advance rather than letting perception drive question formation.

Within this batch, this paper sits alongside two other AI-Scientist-adjacent systems reviewed this session: Faraday/Replica ([2608.13331.md](2608.13331.md)), which RL-trains an agent to replicate published figures under a rubric-based LLM judge, and Spark-to-Paper ([2608.11924.md](2608.11924.md)), which automates end-to-end paper writing with deterministic integrity gates on a frozen backbone. All three share a recurring structural idea — pairing an open-ended LLM agent loop with deterministic, code-enforced checks rather than trusting model self-assessment — but OmniScientist and Spark-to-Paper both stop short of the human validation that the Faraday paper carries out (two pre-registered human studies, one explicitly reported as non-significant). OmniScientist's judge validation, by contrast, checks only the judges' agreement with each other and their freedom from length/self-preference bias, not their agreement with human assessors — a gap worth naming explicitly given how central judge-based scoring is to its central claim.

## Critical discussion

The paper's transparency about its own failure modes is a genuine strength: reporting exactly which checks fired, how often, and on which runs (Table 15) gives a falsifiable, specific account of where an autonomous science pipeline goes wrong (result-selection spin far more than fabrication), rather than a vague assurance that "checks were in place." The provenance/anti-HARKing mechanism is also demonstrated, not just designed — a single fabrication rejection across 36 runs is consistent with the checks doing real work rather than never firing.

Three things limit how far the central "perception is essential" claim can be pushed. First, the perception-vs-blind ablation, the paper's key causal evidence, covers only 5 paired cases — a workable qualitative demonstration (and the mechanism-level analysis in Table 7 is genuinely illuminating), but a thin quantitative base for the headline "wins 85% of comparisons" framing given how much of the paper's contribution claim rests on it (`narrow-evaluation`). Second, OmniScientist is never benchmarked head-to-head against any of the actual prior AI-Scientist systems it positions itself against (The AI Scientist, Agent Laboratory, Kosmos, Co-Scientist) on the same tasks; the comparison that exists is entirely against the authors' own blind ablation of their own system, so the paper's implicit claim of being better than the workflow-complete-but-evidence-incomplete prior systems is argued in Related Work but not measured empirically (`weak-baseline`). Third, and most consequentially, every quality claim in the paper — the 6.3 composite score, the backbone comparison, the perception-ablation gains — is scored exclusively by LLM judges whose internal consistency is checked but whose correspondence to human scientific judgment is never tested; the paper's own novelty-language check (tempering "first"/"never explored" claims because a bounded literature search cannot support them) shows the authors are alert to overclaiming from limited evidence, but no analogous caution is applied to the judge-based evaluation itself, which is the paper's actual measurement instrument (`missing-control`).

What remains well supported after these caveats: OmniScientist reliably completes a diverse 36-case, 12-modality, 5-discipline suite end-to-end with strong backbones, its code-enforced checks demonstrably catch and correct specific classes of methodological malpractice (selective reporting far more often than fabrication), and the case studies show a coherent mechanistic story of how a raw-evidence observation can convert into a statistically validated, robustness-checked finding. Whether the resulting papers represent genuinely higher scientific quality by any standard other than the paper's own LLM judges is not yet established.
