## What this paper is about

Most of what theoretical physics believes is not proved in the mathematician's sense; it rests on arguments the community trusts because independent routes agree and because it matches experiment, not on a rigorous derivation from first principles. The Seiberg-Witten solution of N=2 supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory is a celebrated example: nobody can prove it, because doing so would require first rigorously constructing the interacting quantum field theory itself, a Clay Millennium-Prize-scale problem. This paper's proposal, illustrated by fully formalizing the simplest (genus-one) case of Seiberg-Witten in the Lean 4 proof assistant, is that a theorem prover can still be useful here: instead of trying to prove the unprovable, state the physical reasoning as a short, explicit, named list of postulates, machine-verify every mathematical consequence of those postulates, and let the software report exactly which named assumptions any given result actually depends on. The residual trust in the result is then concentrated onto that short, inspectable list rather than spread diffusely across an entire physics argument, and the paper argues this specific discipline is what validating AI-generated physics results should look like as AI-produced derivations become larger and harder for a human to audit line by line.

## Extended summary

The paper opens by distinguishing two kinds of formalization a physics result can call for: "mathematical physics" formalization, where a rigorous proof already exists and one simply machine-checks it, and "foundations for physical reasoning," where no proof is within reach and the goal is instead to codify the non-rigorous argument the community already trusts. Seiberg-Witten falls squarely in the second category. The paper enforces a strict architectural separation between two kinds of trusted input: "mathematical axioms" (true classical mathematics, assumed without a from-scratch Lean proof, and enforced to name only mathematical objects, never physics vocabulary) and "physical postulates" (formal translations of physical assumptions, carried as predicates in a theorem's type signature rather than as global axioms). A dictionary fixes every physical concept as a named mathematical object (e.g., the physical "coupling tau" is defined to be exactly the mathematical "period ratio," and this identification is never made silently), so that physics only ever enters through this explicit dictionary and eight named hypotheses, H0 through H7 (the carrier data of the low-energy abelian gauge theory; special-Kähler geometry from N=2 supersymmetry; BPS-state singularities; Picard-Lefschetz monodromy from massless charged states; electric-magnetic duality; a discrete R-symmetry; one-loop-exact weak-coupling asymptotics; and spurionic covariance tying together the family of theories parameterized by the dynamical scale Lambda).

For the genus-one (SU(2)) case, the paper reports the headline result, existence and uniqueness up to an electric-magnetic duality frame of the effective coupling, as a fully machine-checked, "sorry-free" Lean theorem, and the `#print axioms` command confirms its entire dependency reduces to exactly three built-in logical axioms plus two purely classical, textbook-citable mathematical facts (the thrice-punctured-sphere uniformization by the modular group Gamma(2), and a lift-uniqueness lemma from covering-space theory) — no additional physics axioms beyond the H0-H7 hypotheses, which enter only as explicit predicates a theorem's type demands, not as items in the trusted base. Around this headline, a substantial mathematical spine is separately proved rather than assumed: the curve's genus, positivity of the special-Kähler metric, the exact location and count of the theory's singularities (derived, not asserted, via a Kodaira-classification argument combined with an anomaly-grading argument that the paper shows is independently necessary to rule out an alternative singularity count that satisfies the naive constraints), the explicit closed-form special coordinates and coupling as elliptic integrals, and their one-loop running. A specific worked example (Argyres-Douglas points, where the theory becomes an interacting superconformal field theory with no Lagrangian description) is derived entirely axiom-free as a consequence of definitions already in hand, rather than needing its own postulate, and the paper explicitly verifies this derived notion is non-vacuous by constructing a concrete matter-deformed case where it is provably nonempty, contrasted with a proof that pure SU(2) never realizes it.

A substantial and unusually candid section (4.4) documents specific instances where the discipline of formalization forced genuine physical or logical subtleties into the open that ordinary physics prose glosses over: an initial definition of the periods as single-valued functions on the moduli space was flagged by external review as secretly vacuous (a single-valued holomorphic map into a bounded domain is forced constant by Liouville's theorem, i.e., a free theory), requiring the data to instead live on a universal cover; several hypotheses (H2, H3, H5, H6) initially type-checked but were logically vacuous ("true by fiat") until deliberately hardened to demand a genuine, falsifiable constraint; and pinning down H5 (the discrete R-symmetry) to "order exactly 2N" as a textbook phrasing suggests turned out to be false as stated on the moduli space itself for rank 1 (SU(2)), because the R-symmetry acts faithfully on the underlying field but only with smaller induced order on the Casimir coordinates that parameterize the physical moduli space, a genuine subtlety the textbook phrasing quietly conflates and that formalization surfaced only because the predicate had to actually be checked rather than merely stated.

Validation of the named axioms and postulates proceeds through several distinct, explicitly ordered checks (Section 5): an independent numerical oracle (a separate mpmath/numpy codebase sharing no code or authorship with the Lean development) ran nine suites totaling 278 checks at 30-40 digit precision, twice catching errors in the checking apparatus itself (a fractional power evaluated on the wrong branch; a cycle-normalization factor of two) before ultimately agreeing with the Lean development to full precision; cross-model and adversarial review, where a proposed set of elliptic-integral axioms that had already passed 62 independent numerical checks at 40-digit precision was still rejected by adversarial model review for three statement-level gaps invisible to numerical sampling alone (a pointwise-existence clause supplying no holomorphic choice function; missing boundary limits; an underspecified covering axiom), leading the paper to draw an explicit methodological distinction that "sampling bounds falsity; statement-level review bounds fitness for purpose"; and a four-part validation protocol specifically for one non-provable "dictionary" transcription (the Kodaira-classification bridge used in the singularity count), which the paper demonstrates was once genuinely falsified during development (a hyperbolic matrix briefly occupied a slot the physics required to be parabolic, passing every property the downstream proofs actually used while still being physically wrong) and only caught by checking invariants that classify the transcribed object, not merely the invariants the existing proofs happened to consume.

The paper is explicit about what remains unfinished. At genus one, the trusted mathematical base is exactly five named, deliberately-kept classical axioms, each described as textbook-citable rather than novel (the Gamma(2) uniformization and its lift-uniqueness companion; two theta-function identities from the Jacobi triple product; and Jacobi's inversion theorem), left unproved in Lean as a matter of prioritization rather than necessity. The higher-genus SU(N) generalization is formalized only as an "axiomatized skeleton" resting on one consolidated, unproved period-geometry input (the Gauss-Manin/variation-of-Hodge-structure layer), explicitly deferred to future work. A further section speculates, in three graded tiers of tractability, on which of the eight physical postulates H0-H7 might themselves eventually be derived from more primitive inputs (the BPS bound and Dirac-Schwinger-Zwanziger charge quantization are assessed as "already within reach" as pure linear algebra and Chern-Weil-type arguments; special geometry, one-loop exactness, and the discrete R-symmetry are assessed as "formalizable but needing infrastructure"; and the actual nonperturbative existence of interacting N=2 super-Yang-Mills as a quantum field theory is assessed as "beyond reach," being exactly as hard as the original unprovable claim). The Acknowledgments disclose that the formalization and paper were developed with heavy use of Claude (Anthropic), and with Gemini (Google) and Codex (OpenAI) for cross-checking, second opinions, and review, alongside two named human physicists who read the manuscript for discussion.

## Learnings

The paper's central, most transferable methodological contribution is architectural rather than physics-specific: enforcing, at the level of a proof assistant's type system rather than only in prose, that physical assumptions are named, contentful predicates carried in a theorem's type (never a global axiom, and hardened until removing them actually breaks something) is a directly reusable discipline for any domain where a community trusts an unprovable-but-well-corroborated argument, not just theoretical physics; the specific heuristic the author reports using to catch vacuous hypotheses, "for every existential inside a hypothesis, ask what stops the witness from being reverse-engineered from the thing being constrained," is a concrete, generalizable check against a subtle and easy-to-miss failure mode in any formalization effort.

The documented distinction between what numerical sampling can and cannot catch, illustrated concretely by a set of axioms that passed 62 independent 40-digit-precision numerical checks yet still contained three statement-level logical gaps (a missing holomorphic-choice witness, missing boundary behavior, an underspecified labeling) that only adversarial, statement-level review caught, is a specific and transferable lesson for anyone validating formalized or AI-generated mathematical content: numerical agreement establishes that a formula evaluates correctly at sampled points, but says nothing about whether the formal statement itself asserts what it needs to, and both kinds of check are needed because they catch different classes of error.

The specific validation protocol built for a transcription that cannot itself be proved correct (the physics-to-mathematics dictionary entry mapping a physical singularity type to a Kodaira classification of the corresponding elliptic fiber) is a reusable template for validating any definitional bridge between an informal domain and a formal one: instantiate the transcription against a known concrete case and check it produces the physically expected values; cross-check its internal numerical consistency against an independent oracle; verify it agrees with an independently-derived result reached by a different route; and, most distinctively, deliberately probe it adversarially by constructing a case designed to satisfy every property the existing proofs happen to consume while still being wrong in a way those proofs don't check, since the paper reports this fourth step is precisely what caught a real, previously undetected error (a hyperbolic matrix passing as parabolic) during the project's own development.

## Verification

Verification is the substantive content of this paper rather than an add-on section, and it operates at several genuinely independent levels: the Lean kernel itself provides a machine-checked guarantee that every theorem's proof is logically valid relative to its declared inputs, and the `#print axioms` command makes this dependency list exhaustive and inspectable for any specific result rather than trusting a prose summary of what was assumed; an independent numerical oracle built with no shared code or authorship provides a second, largely orthogonal check on computable content, and the paper reports (rather than merely claims) two specific instances where this oracle caught real errors in its own checking apparatus before ultimately validating the Lean development; and adversarial cross-model review provides a third check aimed specifically at statement-level correctness (does the formal predicate actually say what the physics requires) rather than numerical correctness, with one concrete, reported instance of catching three real gaps that had already survived 62 independent numerical checks. The paper is also unusually explicit that a numerical pass is "necessary, not sufficient," correctly scoping what each verification layer can and cannot establish rather than treating any single check as decisive.

The main limitation of this triangulated verification approach, which the paper does not itself foreground as a caveat but which follows directly from its own Acknowledgments, is that the models used for cross-checking and adversarial review (Claude, Gemini, Codex) are largely the same set of AI systems used to help generate the formalization in the first place, so the "independence" between the generating and checking layers is independence across different vendors' models rather than independence from AI-assisted reasoning as a category; a systematic blind spot shared across current-generation frontier LLMs on a particular class of formal or physical reasoning would not necessarily be caught by this specific protocol, even though the paper does report concrete, non-trivial instances where one model's proposed content was caught and rejected by another's adversarial review. Beyond the AI-model layer, the paper names only two human physicists as having read the manuscript for discussion, and does not report an independent human formal-methods expert auditing the Lean proof scripts themselves beyond the author's own review, so the ultimate human-expert check on the formalization's fidelity to the informal Seiberg-Witten argument rests substantially on the author's own judgment plus the disclosed AI-assisted cross-checking.

## Field context

This paper is an explicit methodological continuation of the author's own prior project, reviewed earlier in this corpus as [reviews/2603.15770.md](2603.15770.md) (Formalization of QFT, cited here directly as the "OSforGFF" project, reference [11]), which used the same AI-assisted, Lean 4-based approach to formalize a rigorously provable result (the free quantum field satisfying the Osterwalder-Schrader axioms) and reported in detail on the practical workflow of using Claude Code, GPT, and Gemini as the primary labor for writing formal proof scripts, including a "backward-chaining" strategy of accepting agent-proposed helper lemmas as tracked axioms specifically to avoid derailing an agent's focus. This paper extends that same author-and-tooling lineage to a genuinely non-rigorous physics result for the first time, and the shift in what "formalization" even means between the two projects, from "prove an already-true theorem" to "make an unprovable argument's assumptions explicit and machine-auditable", is itself a natural and instructive progression to read the two papers together against: the earlier paper's reported AI-tooling failure modes (confidently misapplied analogies from more familiar mathematical settings; unreliable AI-generated documentation; long unproductive search on hard steps) describe the mechanics of using AI to build a formal proof, while this paper describes what becomes possible, and what new validation discipline becomes necessary, once that same tooling is turned on a result where no proof exists to build toward in the first place. The paper also situates itself within a broader, explicitly cited wave of interactive-theorem-proving-for-physics work (HepLean/PhysLean, and a concurrent SU(5) model-building formalization it calls "adjacent in spirit"), distinguishing its own contribution as codifying non-rigorous physical reasoning as auditable postulates, rather than formalizing an already-rigorous physics derivation or a model-building search.

## Critical discussion

The paper's central methodological claim, that separating machine-checked mathematical consequences from explicitly named physical postulates concentrates and shrinks the trust required in an otherwise-unprovable physics result, is demonstrated concretely rather than only argued for: the genus-one headline result's complete, machine-verified dependency list (three logical axioms plus two classical covering-theory facts) is a specific, checkable, and genuinely small trusted base for a celebrated and structurally intricate physics result, and the paper's own documented instances of catching vacuous hypotheses and a real physical subtlety (the R-symmetry's order on the moduli space versus on the underlying field) are concrete evidence that the discipline does real diagnostic work rather than merely re-packaging an already-understood argument in new notation.

Two points bound how far the paper's claims should be read. First, the fully machine-verified result is specifically the simplest genus-one (SU(2)) case; the more general and physically richer SU(N) family, which is what most of the wider Seiberg-Witten literature actually studies and applies, remains an "axiomatized skeleton" resting on one substantial, unproved consolidated period-geometry axiom, so the paper's demonstration of a small, fully-closed trusted base is established for one specific instance of the broader family rather than for Seiberg-Witten theory in general; the paper is explicit about this scope, describing the higher-genus case as future work rather than presenting it as already achieved. Second, the verification protocol's "independence" is independence across different AI vendors' models rather than independence from AI-assisted reasoning as a category, since the same set of tools (Claude, Gemini, Codex) both helped construct the formalization and served as the cross-checking and adversarial reviewers; the paper's own reported successes (three real statement-level gaps caught by adversarial review after passing extensive numerical checks) are genuine evidence this specific protocol catches real errors, but they do not establish that the protocol would catch an error class that multiple frontier LLMs share a common blind spot on, a possibility the paper does not itself address.

What remains well supported after these caveats: the genus-one formalization's specific, machine-audited dependency list is a real, verifiable artifact rather than an assertion, and the paper's broader argument, that this discipline (explicit named postulates, machine-checked consequences, and multi-layered independent validation of the postulates themselves) is a more tractable and more honestly-scoped standard for auditing AI-generated physics derivations than either full re-proof or unstructured human line-by-line reading, is well-motivated by the specific, concrete failure modes (vacuous hypotheses, a falsified transcription, numerically-clean-but-logically-gapped axioms) the paper documents encountering and catching during its own development.
