Citation: Yu, X. DualityCert: Verifier-Gated Language-Model Repair of Broken Duality Claims in Quantum Field Theory. arXiv preprint, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23614v2

## What this paper is about

Seiberg duality, the claim that two different-looking four-dimensional supersymmetric gauge theories describe the same low-energy physics, is exactly the kind of result theoretical physics has no proof assistant for: it isn't derived from a fixed set of axioms, and physicists instead judge it by whether a battery of consistency checks (matching anomalies, matching charges, matching protected data) all come out consistent. This paper builds DualityCert, a symbolic verifier that runs exactly those consistency checks as fast, exact, mechanical computations, then uses it to construct a controlled experiment: give a language-model agent a duality claim that has been deliberately broken, let it edit the claim over several rounds while the verifier checks each attempt, and measure, under a fully preregistered statistical protocol, which specific ways of using that cheap verifier (retrying with feedback, versus just resampling many independent attempts and keeping the first one that passes) actually work best, and whether the answer is the same for every language model tested.

## Extended summary

A candidate duality claim in DualityCert is a pair of quiver gauge theories (nodes with gauge ranks, arrows carrying R-charges, and a superpotential), encoded in a small JSON schema. The verifier checks the claim against a registry of consistency obligations grouped into four families: 't Hooft anomaly matching (a one-loop-exact, renormalization-group-invariant quantity that must agree between genuinely dual theories), superpotential R-charge consistency (every superpotential term must be gauge-invariant and carry R-charge exactly two), central-charge matching (computed from the encoded R-symmetry, without performing the a-maximization that would determine the true superconformal R-charge), and a bounded classical proxy for the chiral ring (comparing gauge-invariant closed-loop operators up to a fixed word-length or R-charge cutoff, since the exact chiral ring is a harder, quantum-corrected object). A claim receives a "consistency certificate" if at least one obligation passes and none fails; the paper is explicit throughout that this states no tested inconsistency was found, not that the duality is proven, and documents precisely where the encoded checks fall short of the actual physics (the implemented SU(2) cubic-anomaly condition is a chirality-balance convention rather than a true anomaly constraint since that anomaly vanishes identically for SU(2); the genuinely subtle Z2 Witten anomaly is not checked at all; and the chiral-ring comparison is a bounded, non-duality-invariant proxy since operator word length itself is not preserved across the duality map).

The benchmark is built from six well-known families of quiver gauge theories arising from D3-branes probing toric Calabi-Yau singularities, each dualized at a chosen node to produce a verified, consistency-certified seed pair, then broken by one of four depth-one perturbations (deleting a superpotential term, flipping a coefficient sign, perturbing an R-charge, or changing a gauge-node rank), keeping only perturbations that the verifier confirms broke the claim (145 fixtures survive out of a larger generated pool, with the attrition explicitly recorded). An agent is given the intact theory, the broken counterpart, and feedback from the previous round, and must return a complete revised theory (not a patch) across up to five rounds; a verbatim copy of the intact theory is rejected by construction to prevent a trivial non-repair. Five policies are compared: a single unaided attempt; content-free retry (only "verification failed"); verifier feedback (the names and categories of failed obligations, but not their numeric values); masked feedback (the same structure with obligation identities replaced by neutral placeholders, isolating whether the specific identity of the feedback matters or only its presence/structure); and independent verifier-filtered resampling (up to eleven independent single-shot attempts, stopping at the first success). A "stop-first strategy portfolio" (single attempt, then retry, then feedback, in sequence, under the same eleven-attempt budget) is constructed after the fact by replaying already-collected data rather than making new model calls, so it can be directly compared against resampling under an identical budget.

The entire analysis protocol, including the five confirmatory endpoints, the statistical model (a binomial generalized-estimating-equation fit with fixture-level clustering to account for the fact that some fixtures are intrinsically harder across every policy, Holm-adjusted for multiple comparisons), and the rules for handling provider-side infrastructure failures, was committed to the paper's repository before the first confirmatory model call and left unmodified. The confirmatory campaign covers DeepSeek-Chat and Qwen-Plus with three replications per policy (a replication count chosen in advance from a minimum-detectable-effect calculation), plus a separately preregistered extension adding MiniMax-M2.5 under its own multiple-comparison correction.

The results show a clear pattern that the paper is careful not to over-generalize. Verifier-gated iteration reliably beats a single unaided attempt on both primary models (+8.3 percentage points on DeepSeek-Chat, +7.1 on Qwen-Plus, both Holm-adjusted p<0.002, with the same sign in every individual replication), and this iteration gain also replicates on the MiniMax-M2.5 extension (+11.5 pp). But which of the two budget-matched exploitation strategies is better reverses completely between models: under the same eleven-attempt budget, the stop-first strategy portfolio loses to independent resampling by 10.3 points on DeepSeek-Chat but wins by 14.7 points on Qwen-Plus (both statistically significant after correction, with consistent per-replication signs), and the extension model's result agrees with DeepSeek-Chat's ordering (portfolio loses to resampling by 9.0 points). The content of the feedback itself only matters, in this study, on one of the two primary models: on Qwen-Plus, structured verifier feedback beats content-free retry by 8.7 points, and interpretable, named obligation identities specifically (as opposed to structurally identical but anonymized placeholders) are worth a further 6.4 points; neither effect is statistically detectable on DeepSeek-Chat, and the feedback-content endpoints are not significant for the MiniMax-M2.5 extension either. The paper's explicit interpretation is non-universality of the tested policies across the specific evaluated models, not a general ranking of models or policies, and it states this limitation itself rather than leaving a reader to infer it.

Several disclosed limitations bound the study's reach. The E4 comparison (portfolio versus resampling) confounds several differences at once (later portfolio attempts see feedback; portfolio attempts are sequentially dependent; the budget is split across three distinct stages rather than eleven independent draws), so the sign reversal cannot, by the paper's own account, be attributed to a single specific mechanism. Invalid or malformed final model outputs were common across every tested model on the feedback-heavy policies (32-45%), a data-quality reality the paper reports rather than filters out. Exploratory depth-two (doubly-perturbed) fixtures push every tested policy on DeepSeek-Chat to near-floor performance (at or below 7% success), indicating the entire positive confirmatory result is confined to a comparatively shallow perturbation regime. The MiniMax-M2.5 extension ran under a provider-forced reasoning mode that could not be disabled, had the highest invalid-response rate of the three models, and required an audited contamination-cleaning procedure (45 records with exogenous transport errors quarantined and rerun under a prespecified, outcome-independent rule), all disclosed with byte-exact preserved records and SHA-256 hashes.

## Learnings

The specific empirical finding that the better of two budget-matched ways to exploit a cheap verifier (iteratively retrying with feedback versus independently resampling and filtering) reverses between two different language models tested under an otherwise identical protocol is a directly transferable methodological caution for anyone designing a verifier-gated agent pipeline: a single model's result for "which search/retry strategy works best against this verifier" should not be assumed to generalize to a different underlying model, and a pipeline intended to work across multiple models may need to select its exploitation strategy per-model rather than adopting one strategy universally.

The masked-feedback control (E5: structurally identical feedback with obligation identities replaced by neutral placeholders, versus the same structure with real obligation names) is a specific, reusable experimental design for isolating whether an agent benefits from the semantic content of feedback or merely from its presence and structure; the paper's own finding, that this premium is detectable on one model but not the other, is itself evidence that this specific confound (a feedback intervention's apparent benefit coming from restructuring the interaction rather than from the information it conveys) is a real, model-dependent risk worth checking for directly rather than assuming feedback content is what matters whenever a feedback intervention shows a gain.

The general design pattern the paper extracts from its own implementation, a serializable claim schema, an exact and finite obligation registry, a certificate that records per-obligation outcomes without concealing what was not checked, a policy-controlled feedback projection, and a final acceptance judge at least as strict as the interaction-time checks, is explicitly offered as reusable for any research area with its own sharp, native consistency checks (the paper lists candidates from the conformal bootstrap, scattering-amplitude factorization, and generalized global symmetries, among others), independent of the specific Seiberg-duality obligations used here; this is a transferable blueprint for building a cheap, exact repair-environment verifier in any similarly axiom-free but consistency-check-rich domain.

## Verification

This paper's verification methodology is unusually rigorous for the size of the study: the full statistical analysis protocol (endpoint definitions, the GEE model specification with fixture-level clustering, the multiplicity correction, and the rules for handling provider-side infrastructure failures) was frozen and committed to the repository, identified by commit hash, before the first confirmatory model call, and the paper states it was not modified afterward. The replication count (R=3) was chosen in advance from a minimum-detectable-effect table rather than post-hoc. Multiple testing is explicitly controlled via a Holm-adjusted primary family, with secondary and descriptive endpoints kept clearly separated from the confirmatory family throughout. Provider-side infrastructure failures are distinguished from genuine model failures via a prespecified, outcome-independent contamination predicate, with affected records quarantined, rerun, and preserved byte-exactly alongside a SHA-256 ledger, rather than silently dropped or cherry-picked; malformed model output, by contrast, is explicitly never excluded and always counts as an outcome, closing off a specific and common source of survivorship bias in agent-evaluation studies. The extension to a third model (MiniMax-M2.5) under its own preregistered, separately-corrected hypothesis family functions as a genuine out-of-sample check on the primary campaign's iteration-gain and portfolio-versus-resampling findings, and it is disclosed as differing from the primary campaigns in specific, material ways (forced reasoning mode, higher invalid-response rate) rather than being presented as a clean third replicate.

The main scope limitation, which the paper itself states directly and repeatedly, is that the confirmatory claim is about the tested policies on the three named models within one duality class in one corner of four-dimensional N=1 gauge theories at one perturbation depth, not a general classification of models or a claim about verifier-gated repair broadly; the exploratory depth-two results, where every tested policy on DeepSeek-Chat falls to near-floor performance, are reported specifically to demonstrate this boundary rather than to claim a harder-difficulty success. The verifier's own certificate is repeatedly and precisely scoped: several named obligations (the SU(2) Witten anomaly, a-maximization, exact chiral-ring comparison beyond the bounded classical proxy) are outside what is checked, and the paper documents this as a stated property of the instrument rather than a discovered flaw, which is itself a strong verification practice, but it does mean that "CERTIFIED" in this paper's technical sense is a narrower guarantee than an unqualified reading of "the duality claim is consistent" might suggest.

## Field context

The paper positions itself explicitly against two adjacent approaches it distinguishes directly: proof-assistant-grounded language-model reasoning (AlphaProof, LeanDojo, AlphaGeometry), which it argues cannot be directly transplanted to physics because formal QFT is not built on a fixed axiomatic foundation the way mathematics is; and its own prior physics-focused evaluation work, cited directly as reference [1] and already reviewed in this corpus as [reviews/2604.14188.md](2604.14188.md) (Grading the Unspoken), which evaluates whether LLMs can reconstruct the tacit, process-level reasoning behind QFT and string-theory results rather than checking a final answer, a complementary diagnosis of the same underlying problem (final-answer matching cannot reveal whether intermediate physics reasoning was sound) that this paper addresses by building an exact mechanical check instead of relying on human or LLM judgment of the reasoning trace. The paper also directly cites and distinguishes itself from two recent Lean-4-based physics-formalization efforts already reviewed in this corpus: [reviews/2603.15770.md](2603.15770.md) (Formalization of QFT) and [reviews/2607.06379.md](2607.06379.md) (Axioms for physical reasoning, on the Seiberg-Witten solution), noting that those projects derive physical results inside a proof assistant while DualityCert instead encodes the consistency checks the field already uses as evidence, without attempting a formal derivation. Its explicitly named "nearest methodological comparison" is [reviews/2604.00149.md](2604.00149.md) (Towards Verifiable and Self-Correcting AI Physicists for Quantum Many-Body Simulations), which the paper distinguishes on the grounds that the cited system verifies an entire research workflow with a combined programming-and-physics verifier, whereas DualityCert verifies a single, fixed-schema claim against a bounded obligation registry, a narrower but more controllable design that is specifically what makes the paper's preregistered policy comparison possible. The paper also flags a genuinely concurrent, independently-motivated paper (Heckman, Meynet, Mininno, and Shiu, cited but not yet in this corpus) that trains neural networks to search for the mutation sequences connecting Seiberg-dual quivers, noting the two approaches are complementary (a learned search could propose candidates for DualityCert's verifier to certify) rather than competing.

## Critical discussion

The paper's statistical and experimental design is a genuine methodological strength relative to most agent-evaluation studies: the preregistration is verifiable by commit hash rather than merely claimed, the fixture-clustered GEE model correctly accounts for the fact that outcomes on the same 145 fixtures are correlated across policies (an intrinsically hard fixture fails under many policies at once), the Holm correction is applied to a clearly-defined primary family rather than to a post-hoc-selected subset of tests, and the extension to a third model under its own separately preregistered correction functions as a real, disclosed replication check rather than an opportunistic addition. The paper's central interpretive move, framing its main finding as "non-universality of the tested policies" rather than overreaching into a general classification of which models or strategies are better, is an appropriately calibrated conclusion given the evidence, and the paper states this scoping explicitly rather than leaving a reader to infer the appropriate caution.

The clearest limitation, which the paper discloses but is worth naming directly, is that the "non-universality" finding rests on comparing exploitation-policy orderings across only three language models, two of which (DeepSeek-Chat and the MiniMax-M2.5 extension) agree with each other while only one (Qwen-Plus) shows the opposite ordering; this is a real and statistically well-supported difference within the tested models, but a reader should understand it as evidence that the ordering is not universal across at least these three models, rather than as evidence about how models in general are likely to split on this question, a distinction the paper itself is careful to draw. The E4 comparison's confound (portfolio and resampling differ in feedback content, sequential dependence, and budget allocation simultaneously) means the paper correctly cannot, and does not claim to, identify which specific ingredient drives the reversal; the E1/E5 feedback-content endpoints provide partial, model-specific evidence toward this question but do not resolve it. The entire confirmatory result is also specific to depth-one perturbations of one class of quiver dualities, with the paper's own depth-two exploratory data showing every tested policy collapsing to near-floor performance, so the demonstrated iteration and exploitation-policy effects should not be assumed to hold at greater task difficulty within even this same narrow physics domain.

What remains well supported after these caveats: the verifier itself is an exact, fast, and precisely-scoped mechanical instrument whose limitations are documented as design choices rather than discovered gaps, and it functions correctly as a repair-loop gate regardless of how the downstream policy-comparison results should be read; the core iteration-gain finding (verifier-gated retry beats a single unaided attempt) is robustly replicated across all three tested models with consistent per-replication signs and Holm-corrected significance, making it the paper's most broadly supported empirical claim; and the demonstrated reversal in which budget-matched exploitation policy performs better is a genuine, carefully-controlled, and honestly-scoped finding about the specific tested models and task, valuable precisely because it resists being oversold into a general claim the evidence does not support.
