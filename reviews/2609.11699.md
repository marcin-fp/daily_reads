Citation: Rongcan Pei, Zhepei Wei, Shuyao Xu, Xinyu Zhu, Wei-Lin Chen, and Yu Meng. Negative Self-Distillation: Learning to Reason by Avoiding Flaws. arXiv preprint, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11699v1

## What this paper is about

On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) lets a language model improve itself without an external teacher by having the model, conditioned on the ground-truth answer, act as its own teacher for its own generated reasoning. But a model that already knows the answer produces artificially confident, overly linear reasoning traces, and forcing the student to imitate that confidence can suppress the uncertainty and self-correction that hard problems actually require. This paper proposes training the model to move away from bad reasoning instead of toward a privileged-information teacher's reasoning: the model generates its own "negative teacher" (itself, prompted to reason carelessly on a given problem, with no ground-truth answer involved anywhere), and training pushes the student's token distribution away from that self-generated negative teacher wherever the divergence looks like a genuine reasoning flaw rather than ordinary language.

## Extended summary

**Method.** Negative Self-Distillation (NSD) has two components. First, negative condition generation: for each unlabeled training question, the student samples an initial solution, then is prompted (conditioned on the question and its own initial trace) to generate an adaptive, question-specific negative instruction (e.g. "reason carelessly by wrongly calculating..."); this online strategy requires no gold answer anywhere in the pipeline. Second, gated unlikelihood training: two frozen copies of the same initial student model are instantiated, a reference model (conditioned only on the question) and a negative teacher (conditioned on the question plus the negative instruction); for each token the student actually sampled, a gate G_t = max(0, negative-teacher-probability minus reference-probability) is computed, so a token is only flagged as a reasoning flaw if the negative condition specifically increased its likelihood relative to the same model's ordinary behavior. Naive unlikelihood training (directly penalizing every gated token) is shown to be unstable — the loss and gradient blow up as the student's own probability for a token approaches 1, which risks unlearning basic linguistic priors like punctuation — so the penalty is squashed through a sigmoid, which the paper's own analysis (Figure 3, over 4,096 tokens from 100 samples) shows redistributes penalty weight toward low-to-mid-probability tokens and away from high-confidence structural tokens. A point-wise forward-KL regularization term against the reference model (a single-sample importance-weighted estimator, not a full-vocabulary computation) anchors the overall update. Only one student rollout per training sample is needed; the reference and negative-teacher forward passes share weights and can be computed in parallel.

**Setup.** Training uses MATH (gold labels discarded even for methods that could use them) on Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B, 2 epochs, evaluated on AIME 2024/2025/2026, HMMT 2025 Feb, AMC 2023, OlympiadBench (open-ended subset, excluding proof problems), and MATH-500, reported as Avg@8 under non-thinking mode (thinking-mode results reported in the appendix). Baselines are OPSD (gold-conditioned full-vocabulary KL distillation, uses ground truth), Intuitor (a GRPO variant using self-certainty/confidence as an intrinsic reward), and TTRL (a GRPO variant using majority-voting pseudo-labels, applied here to the training set rather than the test set for a fair comparison).

**Main results.** NSD achieves the largest average improvement (Delta Avg over the untrained base model, averaged over all seven benchmarks) at every model size tested: +2.3% (1.7B), +7.5% (4B), +6.0% (8B), versus the next-best baseline's average gain in each size group (OPSD's or Intuitor's, which vary by size and are themselves inconsistent across benchmarks, e.g. OPSD wins AIME24 outright at 1.7B and 8B but has negative or near-zero average gain at 8B). The paper reports 95% confidence intervals and one-sided p-values for each Delta Avg (computed, per the table note, as the probability of observing an improvement at least this large by chance); NSD's intervals exclude zero at every model size (p < 0.001, 0.0001, 0.0001 for 1.7B/4B/8B respectively), while several baselines' intervals include zero or near-zero at multiple sizes (e.g. Intuitor at 1.7B: -0.5% average, 95% CI [-1.8, +0.8]).

**Reflection behavior.** On Qwen3-4B, the frequency of reflection tokens (e.g. "Wait") per response, averaged across AIME24/25 and HMMT25, drops sharply under OPSD (2.2, from a 3.6 baseline) and especially Intuitor (0.8), while NSD raises it to 7.5, roughly double the untrained baseline. The paper interprets this as direct evidence that gold-conditioned or confidence-driven self-supervision suppresses exploratory self-correction, while penalizing self-identified flaws (rather than rewarding confident correctness) encourages the model to keep re-checking its own work.

**Ablations.** Removing the KL regularization term (NSD-noKL) causes a mid-training collapse on Qwen3-4B: the forward-KL curve oscillates sharply and the mean gate-activation value drops precipitously around step 120, which the authors interpret as the gate spuriously firing once the student has drifted too far from the reference model without the KL anchor holding it back. A style-task token ratio metric (lower is better, following a cited prior framework distinguishing "style" tokens from "task" tokens) shows NSD's gating mechanism alone achieves a lower ratio (2.6x-3.5x depending on the negative-conditioning variant) than entropy-based OPSD weighting (3.9x) or vanilla OPSD's loss-based weighting (5.4x), i.e. NSD's gate concentrates training signal on task-relevant tokens more effectively than the compared alternatives. Three negative-conditioning strategies are compared on Qwen3-4B (default online solution-aware generation, an offline solution-aware variant, a question-only offline variant, and a fully static "wiki-irr" variant that conditions on irrelevant Wikipedia text as noise instead of a self-generated instruction); the question-only and even the wiki-irr variants perform comparably to the default online strategy (7.3% and 6.6% average gain vs. 7.8% default), while the offline solution-aware variant underperforms, attributed to relying on stale, outdated self-generated solutions.

**Efficiency.** NSD requires a single rollout per sample (versus 8 for the GRPO-based baselines Intuitor and TTRL) and only three scalar token probabilities per step rather than full-vocabulary logit alignment (OPSD uses top-128 logits; NSD uses top-32). Measured wall-clock time per training step on 6-8 A100 GPUs: NSD-online 68s, NSD-wiki-irr 54s, OPSD 105s, Intuitor 187s.

## Learnings

Constructing a "negative teacher" by prompting the same base model to adopt an explicit adversarial persona (a question-specific "careless reasoner" instruction), rather than by contrasting the model's own low-confidence tokens against nothing, is a distinct self-supervision mechanism from other label-free approaches: it produces a genuine contrastive signal (negative-teacher-probability vs. reference-model-probability, both from frozen copies of the same weights) rather than relying on the student's raw uncertainty alone, and the paper shows this signal can be constructed just as effectively from a static, content-free negative condition (irrelevant Wikipedia text) as from an elaborate self-generated adversarial persona, which suggests the mechanism's real work is being done by the gating and penalty formulation rather than by the sophistication of the negative condition itself.

A simple positive-part gate (max(0, negative-teacher-probability minus reference-probability)) is a cheap, interpretable way to separate "this token is a genuine reasoning flaw specific to the injected negative condition" from "this token is just an ordinary high-probability linguistic token that both models agree on," and the paper's own comparison (Table 3, style-task ratio) suggests this two-model-probability-difference gate concentrates penalty on task-relevant tokens more effectively than an entropy-based or vanilla-OPSD weighting scheme applied to the same underlying signal.

An unbounded unlikelihood objective explodes in gradient magnitude precisely on the highest-confidence tokens (as student probability approaches 1, -log(1-p) approaches infinity), which is a specific, mechanistically-explained reason (not just an empirically observed instability) why naive divergence training on LLM token distributions needs an explicit bound; a sigmoid squashing is one concrete, reusable fix for this exact problem whenever a training objective's penalty term is defined via a log-probability that can approach a singularity.

## Verification

The paper reports 95% confidence intervals and one-sided p-values for every headline Delta Avg number (Table 1), explicitly stating the p-value's meaning (probability the observed improvement arose by chance) rather than leaving significance testing implicit; this is a stronger evidentiary standard than most single-point-estimate comparisons and directly supports the claim that NSD's gains are more consistent than the baselines', several of whose confidence intervals include zero. The KL ablation (Section 5.2) is a genuine causal check, not just a reported hyperparameter sweep: it identifies a specific training-dynamics failure (mid-training collapse, gate misfiring) that occurs when the regularization term is removed, tying the component to a concrete mechanism rather than only a final-score difference. The reflection-token-frequency measurement (Table 2) operationalizes a specific behavioral claim (self-correction is preserved, not just accuracy) with a countable proxy metric, though it is one specific proxy (frequency of a defined token set, detailed in Appendix E) rather than a direct measure of whether the reflection is substantively load-bearing for correctness in each instance. What is not verified in the extracted text: whether the gating mechanism's token-level flags actually correspond to genuine reasoning errors when checked against a ground-truth or human-labeled set of flawed reasoning steps, as opposed to being validated only indirectly through the downstream accuracy and style-task-ratio metrics.

## Field context

NSD sits in the on-policy distillation line of work it reviews in Section 6, building directly on and departing from On-Policy Distillation (OPD, which uses an external, typically stronger teacher) and On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD, which replaces the external teacher with the model itself conditioned on gold-answer hints), and it explicitly connects to unlikelihood training (Welleck et al.) and other negative-signal training work (cited NSR, which explores RLVR driven exclusively by negative signals). Within this corpus, "[Does On-Policy Distillation Really Distill?](2608.31046.md)" (OPSA, reviewed in the previous batch, Purdue) diagnoses closely related ground: it shows empirically that an OPD teacher's token-level supervision is substantially noisy and that the student's improvement is barely affected by that noise, tracing OPD's real benefit to suppressing a small set of low-probability, high-entropy tokens the student itself sampled, and proposes a teacher-free replacement (OPSA) built entirely from the student's own token statistics with no explicit negative-conditioning step. NSD and OPSA converge on the same high-level conclusion, that OPD/OPSD-style privileged-information distillation degrades exploratory, self-correcting reasoning and that a label-free alternative built from the model's own signals can outperform it, but they instantiate the correction mechanism differently: OPSA suppresses the student's own lowest-probability sampled tokens with an entropy-scaled fixed negative advantage and needs no separate negative-conditioning step, while NSD explicitly constructs a contrastive "negative teacher" via a self-generated adversarial persona (or, per its ablation, an even simpler static negative condition) and gates the penalty by comparing that negative teacher's probabilities against a reference model's. Both papers independently measure and report suppressed self-correction behavior as the specific failure mode their method targets and restores, which is a genuine, non-coincidental convergence between two concurrently developed methods rather than a shared citation relationship (neither paper cites the other).

## Critical discussion

The comparison against OPSD, Intuitor, and TTRL is reasonably fair: all methods are evaluated at three model scales, on the same seven benchmarks, with the same training data (MATH, gold labels withheld from the label-free methods), and the paper reports confidence intervals and significance tests rather than only point estimates, which lets a reader distinguish NSD's consistently significant gains from several baselines' gains that are statistically indistinguishable from zero at some scales. The KL ablation and the style-task-ratio comparison each isolate a specific mechanistic claim with a dedicated experiment rather than resting on the main results table alone, which is good practice for a paper introducing several interacting design choices (negative conditioning, gating, sigmoid bounding, KL regularization) at once.

The main limitation is evaluation breadth. Every benchmark tested is competition or textbook mathematics (AIME, HMMT, AMC, OlympiadBench, MATH-500), the single training dataset is MATH, and all three model sizes tested are from the same Qwen3 family; the paper does not report results on non-math reasoning, non-Qwen model families, or larger models, so whether the method's specific advantage (preserving reflection under label-free training) transfers outside competition mathematics or beyond an 8B-parameter, single-family setting is untested (`narrow-evaluation`). The reflection-token-frequency metric is a reasonable and directly interpretable proxy for the paper's central behavioral claim, but a higher raw count of tokens like "Wait" does not by itself establish that each instance of self-correction is substantively productive rather than superficial hedging; the paper's case studies (cited in Appendix D.4 but not included in the extracted main text) are offered as qualitative support for this distinction, which the main text does not independently verify at scale. The paper's stated self-limitation, that NSD depends on the student model's own capacity to generate a meaningful negative condition and may be less effective for very small or weak models, is a genuine and specific caveat the authors raise themselves rather than one this review needs to surface; it is also consistent with the reported pattern that Delta Avg is smallest at 1.7B (+2.3%) and larger at 4B and 8B (+7.5%, +6.0%), though the paper frames this as a scaling benefit of higher-quality self-generated conditions rather than fully separating it from other possible causes (e.g. a smaller model simply having less headroom to improve, or the wiki-irr ablation's finding that even a content-free negative condition performs comparably, which somewhat complicates the specific causal story that self-generated condition quality is what drives the scaling pattern).

What remains well supported after these caveats: the central comparative claim, that NSD outperforms OPSD, Intuitor, and TTRL in average accuracy gain at all three tested model sizes with statistically significant confidence intervals, is directly evidenced rather than asserted; the KL-ablation-demonstrated necessity of the regularization term and the sigmoid-bounding's role in preventing gradient explosion on high-confidence tokens are each supported by a targeted mechanistic experiment rather than by the aggregate result alone; and the finding that NSD preserves or increases reflection-token frequency while OPSD and Intuitor suppress it is a specific, measured behavioral contrast rather than an unquantified impression.
