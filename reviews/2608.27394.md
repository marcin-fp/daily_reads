Citation: Maayan Sharon and Tom Hope. RATIO: A Benchmark for Retrieval Across Typed Ideation Operations in Scientific Literature. arXiv preprint, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27394v3

## What this paper is about

When a researcher (or an AI system doing literature-grounded ideation) looks for inspiration in prior work, "relevant" isn't one thing: a paper might suggest a direct fix for a stated problem, reframe it more generally, or offer a concrete worked instance of a general principle. Standard scientific-retrieval benchmarks only test topical relevance — does this document answer the query — which can't tell these apart. RATIO introduces three typed "ideation moves" (ADDRESS: retrieve an approach to a stated problem; BROADEN: retrieve a more general formulation; SPECIFY: retrieve a concrete instantiation) as distinct retrieval tasks, built at corpus scale from millions of full-text CS papers using authors' own discourse markers as supervision.

## Extended summary

**Construction.** The core trick is extending discourse-marker distant supervision (previously used only for sentence-pair classification) to large-scale retrieval. When a sentence begins with a phrase like "To address this issue," "More generally," or "Specifically," that phrase is treated as author-provided ground truth that the following sentence stands in the corresponding relation (ADDRESS/BROADEN/SPECIFY) to the preceding one; the marker is then stripped and only the (query, gold) sentence pair is kept. Building the marker lexicon was itself a multi-stage effort: manual curation (two NLP experts independently checked 15 sampled pairs per candidate marker, keeping only markers that clearly and unambiguously signaled the target relation and referred to the immediately preceding sentence — ruling out markers like "Therefore," used in prior NLI work, and non-anaphoric markers like "In general,"), LLM expansion (SOTA LLMs generated and self-rated candidate markers, keeping only "strong-true" ones, spot-checked by an expert on 1,500 samples), and rule-based pattern expansion (Hearst-style templates instantiated from curated term lists, validated by an LLM). The final lexicon of 4,252 markers was matched against ~366.6M consecutive sentence pairs from a large full-text CS corpus; 809 markers actually fired, yielding ~3M pairs across 1.1M papers, and two NLP experts vetted all 809 firing markers as valid.

**Splits and negatives.** A strict temporal split (train: papers 2015-Sept 2025; validation: Oct-Dec 2025; test: Jan-May 2026, all published after every evaluated model's release) prevents both train/test leakage and pretraining contamination. The candidate pool is deliberately not exhaustive-corpus or random-negative: about 80% of the ~13.8M training candidates are "distractor" sentences captured by markers that were considered and explicitly rejected during curation (contrast markers, non-anaphoric generalizers, etc.), chosen because the authors found that random unrelated sentences as negatives inflated results in preliminary experiments. The final dataset totals just over 3 million (query, gold) pairs, overwhelmingly SPECIFY (2.78M) with much smaller ADDRESS (222,707) and especially BROADEN (15,592) counts — reflecting how much less often authors use broadening discourse markers in practice.

**Silver test set.** Because training labels are noisy distant supervision, the primary evaluation set is built with heavier validation: two LLM judges independently check grammar/coherence/self-containment and then enforce the relation itself (e.g., for ADDRESS, the query must be phrased as a problem and the gold must directly mitigate it; direction is strictly enforced for BROADEN/SPECIFY). Judge prompts were calibrated against 300 expert annotations per relation (keeping the two best-scoring of 4-6 candidate prompts per relation), reaching F1 0.83-0.87 (ADDRESS), 0.86-0.87 (SPECIFY), and 0.76 (BROADEN) against experts, with five-expert inter-annotator F1 of 0.87/0.90/0.82 respectively — the prompt-expert agreement is comparable to expert-expert agreement on what the paper itself calls an intrinsically difficult annotation task. The resulting silver test set has 17,579 queries.

**Retrieval experiments.** Three dense retrievers (all-mpnet-base-v2, ModernBERT-embed-large, Stella-en-1.5B-v5, all pretrained on scientific text) plus BM25 are evaluated pretrained and after relation-specific contrastive fine-tuning (15 fine-tuned variants total, ~1,800 GPU-hours). Pretrained models barely beat BM25. Fine-tuning yields large relative gains (1.6-2.4x MRR@10 for the best model, ModernBERT-embed-large, which leads on every operation on the silver set) but absolute performance stays low across the board — even the best model's MRR@100 exceeds its MRR@10 by at most 0.8 points, meaning near-misses aren't the failure mode; the model is often just wrong. An oracle ablation restricting the candidate pool to only the query's own operation-type candidates confirms fine-tuned models retain large gains even without the option of using operation-style as a shortcut, ruling out the concern that fine-tuning just teaches superficial discourse-style pattern-matching.

**Beyond the single mined positive.** A separate, human-calibrated LLM judge independently evaluates each of the top-10 retrieved candidates per query (1,200 queries, 24K pairs, 48K judgments under two prompts with hard/soft agreement), without ever showing the judge the mined gold, rank, or source model. Under hard agreement, the tuned ModernBERT-embed-large gets at least one accepted candidate in the top 10 for 89.0% of SPECIFY and 76.5% of ADDRESS queries (vs. 65.5% and 40.5% for the untuned baseline), but only 29.0% of BROADEN queries (up from 19.3%). Crucially, the mined "gold" is frequently not the accepted candidate: for tuned ADDRESS, 41.2% of queries have an LLM-accepted alternative in the top 10 despite the original mined positive being absent, and 88-90% of all accepted non-gold top-10 candidates come from a different paper than the query — recomputing MRR@10 to count any accepted candidate (not just the single mined gold) raises ADDRESS from 24.5 to 41.2. A final pairwise LLM-judged "inspiration potential" comparison (fine-tuned vs. baseline ranked lists, position-bias-controlled by swapping list order) prefers the fine-tuned retriever's list for all three operations, most strongly for ADDRESS.

## Learnings

Extending discourse-marker distant supervision from sentence-pair classification (its traditional use) to corpus-scale retrieval is a specific, demonstrated methodological move: because the marker is stripped and the model must retrieve the gold sentence out of millions of candidates rather than merely classify a given pair, the resulting supervision signal forces the retriever to encode the relation itself rather than surface lexical cues correlated with the marker — validated directly by the oracle-filtering ablation showing gains persist even when style-based shortcuts are unavailable.

The finding that single-mined-positive metrics substantially underestimate a retriever's real performance (ADDRESS MRR@10 goes from 24.5 to 41.2 once any judge-accepted candidate counts, not just the discourse-mined one) is a concrete, quantified instance of a general caution for any retrieval benchmark built from naturally occurring rather than exhaustively annotated positives: a low score can reflect the metric's incompleteness rather than the model's failure, and complementary candidate-level judgment is necessary to tell the two apart.

Deliberately populating the negative/candidate pool with near-miss distractors (sentences captured by markers that were considered and rejected for the target relation, rather than random corpus sentences) is a reusable hard-negative-mining technique for any relation-typed retrieval task: the paper's own preliminary experiments showed random negatives inflate apparent performance, and rejected-marker distractors force the retriever to distinguish the target relation from superficially similar discourse functions (contrast, continuation, generic elaboration) rather than merely from off-topic noise.

## Verification

Central and layered throughout construction and evaluation: marker validity is checked at three stages (manual expert review with a strict double-condition test, LLM self-rating kept only at "strong-true," and a final independent two-expert vetting pass on every marker that actually fired in the corpus); the silver test set's LLM-judge prompts are explicitly calibrated against human expert annotations with reported F1 agreement, not merely asserted to be reliable; and the top-10 candidate-level evaluation and the paired inspiration-potential comparison both use the same human-calibrated judge prompts, applied blind to the mined gold, rank, and source model, with position-bias control via prompt-order swapping. The oracle operation-filtering ablation is itself a targeted verification step against a specific alternative explanation (shallow style-shortcut learning) rather than a generic ablation. The main residual uncertainty the paper itself surfaces is IR's standard false-negative problem (a candidate can be a valid inspiration without being the one sentence the author happened to write next), which motivated the entire top-10 judging exercise rather than resting on the silver set's single-positive ranking alone.

## Field context

RATIO extends a line of work using literature to support ideation and hypothesis generation (Hope et al.'s functional concept graphs; CHIMERA, Sternlicht and Hope 2026, which mines naturally occurring idea recombinations) and directly extends MIR (Garikaparthi et al. 2025), the closest prior benchmark, from paper-level, abstract-only methodology-inspiration retrieval to full-text, sentence-level retrieval decomposed into three typed operations. It explicitly distinguishes itself from topical scholarly-retrieval benchmarks like LitSearch, and from prior discourse-marker-supervised NLI resources (SciNLI/MSciNLI), which only classify given sentence pairs under a much smaller, entailment-oriented marker set rather than retrieving from a large corpus. Within this corpus, RATIO shares a senior author (Tom Hope) and an explicit citation relationship with MUSE ([2608.10974.md](2608.10974.md), reviewed earlier in this batch), which mines full-text Problem-Solution-Rationale triplets from the same broad line of scientific-NLP work at Hebrew University/AI2; both papers cite CHIMERA as the same lab's own prior work on idea recombination, and both apply a similar template of scaling a knowledge-mining task from a small expert-annotated seed to a large, automatically-constructed corpus with heavy LLM- and human-validated quality control. RATIO's ADDRESS/BROADEN/SPECIFY relations and MUSE's Problem-Solution-Rationale triplets are complementary rather than overlapping constructs — RATIO retrieves candidate inspirations at different levels of abstraction, while MUSE extracts problem-solving structure already realized within a single paper — so this is a genuine, lab-level methodological connection rather than a redundant or conflicting one.

## Critical discussion

The benchmark construction is unusually rigorous for a distantly-supervised resource: marker validity is checked at multiple independent stages, the temporal split is specifically designed against both train/test leakage and LLM pretraining contamination, and the negative-sampling design is justified by a documented preliminary finding (random negatives inflate results) rather than asserted by convention. The top-10 candidate-level judging and the resulting "mined positive is not the only valid inspiration" finding are a genuine strength: rather than reporting only silver-set ranking metrics and leaving IR's well-known false-negative problem as an unaddressed caveat, the paper directly measures and quantifies how much the single-positive metric underestimates true performance, which is a higher standard of self-scrutiny than most retrieval-benchmark papers apply to their own evaluation protocol.

Two things temper how evenly the reported results generalize across the benchmark's own three operations. First, BROADEN is dramatically underpowered relative to SPECIFY and ADDRESS — 15,592 total pairs versus 2.78 million and 222,707 respectively, with only 1,410 test queries and 584 silver-test queries — and its results are consistently and substantially the weakest across every table (lowest fine-tuned MRR@10, lowest Top-10 hit rate at 29.0% even after tuning, lowest judge-prompt agreement with experts at F1 0.76-0.82 versus 0.86-0.90 for the other two relations). This imbalance reflects a genuine fact about how researchers write (broadening markers are simply rarer than specifying or problem-addressing ones), which the paper does not hide, but it means the BROADEN numbers throughout the paper rest on a much smaller and noisier evidence base than the headline SPECIFY/ADDRESS results, and conclusions specifically about BROADEN retrievability should be read with more caution than the other two operations (`narrow-evaluation`). Second, despite the abstract's framing as covering "scientific literature," the benchmark is constructed entirely from CS papers; the authors state this explicitly and argue the methodology generalizes to other domains and relations, but this generalization claim is not tested anywhere in the paper, so RATIO's demonstrated results describe CS-literature retrieval specifically (`narrow-evaluation`).

What remains well supported after these caveats: relation-specific fine-tuning produces large, consistent, and multiply-validated gains over generic pretrained retrievers on all three operations (confirmed independently by silver-set ranking metrics, an oracle-filtering ablation against shortcut learning, and blind candidate-level LLM judging), and the finding that tuned retrievers surface valid cross-paper inspirations beyond the single discourse-mined gold sentence is directly demonstrated rather than inferred. The task itself remains, in the paper's own words, "far from solved" — absolute performance is low even for the strongest model and operation, which is a fair and unhedged characterization of where the field stands rather than an overclaim.
