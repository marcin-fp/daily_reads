## What this paper is about

Most models that compete on hard math and agentic-search benchmarks are either hundred-billion-parameter systems or released only as opaque weights, with their training recipes kept proprietary. This technical report presents ZGCM-1, a 7.39B dense model trained entirely from scratch on an efficient, fully open pipeline (architecture, code, data recipes, intermediate checkpoints, and training logs are all released), built on the premise that a small model can compensate for limited parametric memory by pairing deliberate step-by-step reasoning with active external tool use (web search, terminal, binary-analysis tools) rather than trying to memorize the open web. The report documents the full training recipe end to end and distills eight specific empirical findings from the process.

## Extended summary

**Architecture.** ZGCM-1 interleaves gated sliding-window attention (SWA, 128-token window) with global attention at a 5:1 local-to-global ratio (27 SWA layers, 5 global layers), following a schedule previously used at production scale by Gemma 3, plus GQA, QK RMSNorm, and Partial RoPE. A controlled architecture sweep at 10B tokens (comparing full attention, FlashAttention-2 GQA, MLA, and SWA at 1:1/3:1/5:1 ratios) selected SWA 5:1 for matching full attention's tail loss (1.93) while reaching the highest throughput (9,566 tokens/s/GPU); the throughput advantage over full attention widens from 1.13x at 4K context to 3.94x at 256K, and the resulting KV cache at 256K context (5.0 GiB) is 6.4x smaller than full attention's (32.0 GiB) and smaller than a comparably-sized linear-attention alternative (7.0 GiB, GDN 3:1).

**Pre-training and mid-training.** General pre-training (0.99T tokens Stage 1, 3.20T tokens Stage 2) combines curated web, academic/OCR, code, mathematics, LaTeX, and specialized reasoning corpora, with cross-stage deduplication. Stage 1 applies lexical-complexity curriculum ordering to general-language text only (not code or math, where surface complexity is shown to be a poor difficulty proxy), lowering coding and math bits-per-byte at a small general-domain BPB cost (Finding 1). Training uses the Muon optimizer with hybrid FP8 precision and TWEO outlier regularization to stabilize FP8 training (Finding 2), sustaining about 60% BF16-equivalent MFU. Mid-training (600.51B sampled tokens from a 2.86T-token deduplicated pool) progressively scales context across 16K, 64K, and 256K stages, reformulating agentic interaction traces into MDP-style state-action supervision and replaying shorter-context data at each stage to preserve earlier-length performance. The paper estimates a roughly 4.2x pre-training time-to-loss improvement over a BF16/AdamW baseline, computed by multiplying four separately-measured factors: hybrid SWA (1.4x at 16K), FP8 precision/systems (1.5x), Muon (1.8x), and Pre-LN (1.1x, from "exploratory 7B evidence") (Finding 3).

**Post-training.** SFT (4,921,933 examples, 96.46% general / 3.54% agentic) applies rule-based structural filtering, a model-based multi-criteria quality grader calibrated against human-annotated pilot data, and strict 8-gram-overlap decontamination against all evaluation sets (samples with over 50% overlap are purged). A controlled comparison shows pruning roughly half the raw SFT candidate pool improves a six-benchmark mean from 67.78 to 68.83, with gains concentrated in BBH (+10.10) (Finding 4). Oversaturating the mixture with long chain-of-thought data is shown to degrade instruction-following, mitigated by iterative mixture calibration (Finding 5); joint general+agentic training outperforms agent-only fine-tuning (Finding 6); and the paper reports that a long-context foundation established during mid-training lets 256K-scale reasoning and agentic behavior be elicited from moderate-length SFT data, avoiding the need for expensive ultra-long SFT trajectories (Finding 7). RL uses GRPO with domain-matched rewards (binary correctness for math, test-pass-fraction for code) plus KL regularization and a length penalty, with difficulty-based prompt filtering against the initial policy.

**Evaluation.** Against six 7B-8B-scale models (DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B, MiniCPM4.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, Olmo-3-7B-Think, MiMo-7B-RL, OpenThinker3-7B) on 20 benchmarks (Table 2), ZGCM-1 leads on several math/reasoning benchmarks (MATH-500 97.13%, AIME 2026 75.00%, HMMT 2025/2026, AGIEval SAT Math, ARC-AGI-1, HumanEval+) but trails on others (AIME 2024/2025 behind DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B; HARDMath-mini, MATH-P-Hard, OlympiadBench, miniF2F, MBPP+, LiveCodeBench v6, MMLU behind at least one comparably-sized model; GPQA-Diamond notably lower at 47.87% vs. 60.10% for the strongest comparator; IFEval also behind Qwen3-8B and Olmo-3-7B-Think, 75.42% vs. 87.40%/88.20%). On agentic web research (Table 3: WebWalkerQA, BrowseComp, GAIA text-only), ZGCM-1 leads WebWalkerQA (63.09%) among the compared models but trails GPT-5 substantially on BrowseComp (19.43% vs. 54.90%) and GAIA (42.52% vs. 76.40%), and trails Claude 4 Sonnet on GAIA (42.52% vs. 68.30%). On a self-constructed Binary Function Search benchmark (stripped-ELF-binary function identification via a Ghidra interface, 50 held-out tasks from projects excluded from training), ZGCM-1 reaches 62% accuracy, ahead of Qwen3-8B (12%) and most comparably-sized or larger models tested except DeepSeek-V4 Flash, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (both 76%) and GLM-5.1 (66%).

**AI-native R&D.** The team reports using AI agents throughout development (data cleaning, cluster management, semi-autonomous experimentation, and a 2,503-probe Atomic Capability Evaluation suite for fast diagnostic feedback during ablations). Nine core contributors independently rated agent autonomy across 11 R&D task categories on a five-level rubric (L1-L5); by team consensus, operational tasks (experimentation/monitoring, deployment) reached L4 (high autonomy within human-defined objectives), while architecture and learning-algorithm design stayed at L2, with only 1 of 99 individual ratings reaching L5 (Finding 8). The paper explicitly labels these "contributor judgments, not standardized measurements."

**Self-stated limitations.** The report itself flags four limitations: bounded closed-book/parametric-knowledge recall at 7.39B dense scale; a verbosity/instruction-following tension from heavy reasoning supervision requiring ongoing calibration; weaker performance on broader long-horizon software engineering (SWE-bench Verified) and unstructured terminal navigation (Terminal-Bench 2.0) than on the more structured agentic tasks it highlights; and brittleness to tool-schema deviations, noisy observations, or search-API latency during multi-step rollouts.

## Learnings

The finding that lexical complexity is a cheap, effective curriculum-ordering signal for general-language pre-training text but actively misleading for code and mathematics (where surface statistics track boilerplate rather than reasoning depth) is a specific, actionable caution for anyone building difficulty-based curricula at corpus scale: a single global difficulty proxy is unlikely to transfer cleanly across domains with qualitatively different notions of difficulty, and applying it selectively (only where validated) is a concrete, reusable design pattern.

The paper's account of RL-scale sampling and reward design is a fairly standard application of GRPO with domain-matched rewards, but its explicit report that oversaturating an SFT mixture with long chain-of-thought data degrades instruction-following, and that this can be corrected by iterative mixture calibration rather than by simply adding more instruction data, is a useful, specific data point on a tension (deep-reasoning supervision vs. strict instruction adherence) that several other post-training papers in this corpus gesture at without quantifying it as directly.

The paper's own framing of "coupling deliberate internal thinking with active external tool use" as a way for a small model to substitute for missing parametric memory is a clean articulation of a design principle that recurs across several agentic and memory papers in this corpus (e.g. writable-memory and retrieval-augmented approaches reviewed elsewhere), applied here specifically to justify a training recipe rather than an inference-time architecture, which is a distinct but related instance of the same underlying idea.

## Verification

The paper reports several concrete, disclosed verification and quality-control mechanisms rather than asserting data or benchmark quality by fiat: a strict 8-gram-overlap decontamination protocol against all downstream evaluation sets (over 50% overlap triggers purging), an SFT quality grader explicitly calibrated against human-annotated pilot benchmarks rather than used unvalidated, and a self-constructed benchmark (Binary Function Search) whose ground truth is a hidden oracle built from unstripped binaries, requiring an exact address match rather than partial credit. The Atomic Capability Evaluation suite mixes deterministic, rule-scored probes with a separate model applying predefined rubrics only where correctness cannot be expressed as an executable rule, which is a disclosed and reasonable division of labor between exact and judged scoring, though the extracted main text does not detail how the rubric-scored probes' own reliability is checked. The AI4AI autonomy assessment (Finding 8, Table 5, Figure 14) is explicitly and appropriately flagged by the authors themselves as "contributor judgments, not standardized measurements," with error bars described as describing inter-contributor variation rather than statistical confidence, a candid disclosure rather than an overstated claim of a validated finding.

## Field context

ZGCM-1 sits in the growing line of fully open (not merely open-weight) foundation model efforts that release training data, code, and intermediate checkpoints alongside final weights, explicitly citing OLMo 3 as a comparison point for its BF16/AdamW baseline and Gemma 3 as precedent for its 5:1 SWA-to-global attention ratio. Its hybrid gated-SWA architecture is a related but distinct design choice from "[Sliding-window beats linear attention](2608.28444.md)" (Microsoft ASG, reviewed earlier in this batch), which argues that a simple, training-free, sink-augmented sliding-window baseline matches or beats post-hoc linear-attention retrofits of already-pretrained models; ZGCM-1 instead trains a gated hybrid SWA architecture from scratch as its primary production backbone rather than retrofitting a pretrained full-attention model, so the two papers arrive at a shared conclusion (sliding-window-style attention is a highly effective, much cheaper alternative to full attention for long-context serving) from different angles (post-hoc retrofit evaluation vs. from-scratch architecture choice), without directly overlapping in method or claim. No paper currently in this corpus reports a from-scratch open training recipe at this level of technical and data-recipe detail, nor a self-reported, task-by-task assessment of AI agent autonomy across an ML training organization's own R&D workflow, so both aspects are new entry points for the corpus rather than extensions of an existing reviewed thread.

## Critical discussion

The report is unusually transparent for a model-release paper: it discloses a full data-recipe breakdown, an explicit decontamination protocol with a stated threshold, a controlled architecture sweep with reported tail-loss and throughput numbers rather than only a final benchmark table, and candid self-labeling of its own AI-autonomy findings as unvalidated contributor judgment rather than a measured result. The released artifacts (code, weights across training stages, data, and W&B logs) are also, as stated, of a different kind than most "open-weight" releases in this space, materially improving reproducibility if the released assets match what is described.

Two aspects of the headline framing deserve scrutiny against the paper's own tables. First, the widely repeated ~4.2x pre-training time-to-loss figure (in the abstract, Finding 3, and the conclusion) is not measured from a single controlled run of the full recipe against a matched full-attention/BF16/AdamW baseline at the same scale and token budget; it is a multiplicative composition of four separately-measured factors (hybrid SWA at 16K, FP8 precision, Muon, and Pre-LN), the last of which the paper itself describes as coming from "exploratory 7B evidence" rather than a matched controlled comparison, and multiplying independently-measured speedups assumes those factors combine multiplicatively with no interaction, an assumption the paper states plainly ("this roughly indicates") but does not test with an end-to-end head-to-head run. Second, the paper's narrative language ("contend with frontier models orders of magnitude larger," "standing toe-to-toe") sits somewhat ahead of what Table 3 itself shows: ZGCM-1 trails GPT-5 by roughly 35 and 34 points on BrowseComp and GAIA respectively, and trails Claude 4 Sonnet by 26 points on GAIA, even though the underlying numbers are fully and transparently reported in the same table (this is a framing issue, not a reporting omission) (`overclaim`). Beyond framing, no confidence interval or run-to-run variance is reported for any benchmark number in Tables 2-4, despite most non-agentic scores being averaged over 32 runs (a design choice that reduces but does not eliminate the need to report resulting variance), and the single-run, 50-task Binary Function Search evaluation has no repeated-sampling or variance estimate at all (`missing-uncertainty`). The 7B-scale comparison in Table 2 is also a genuinely mixed picture rather than a clean win: ZGCM-1 leads on several math benchmarks but trails noticeably on GPQA-Diamond and IFEval, which the paper's own Limitations section (closed-book knowledge bound, instruction-adherence-vs-verbosity tension) already acknowledges as expected trade-offs of its specific design choices, which is a point in favor of the report's overall candor even where the top-line framing oversells the comparison.

What remains well supported after these caveats: the individually-measured architecture and systems ablations (the SWA-ratio sweep, the KV-cache comparison against a matched linear-attention baseline, the FP8/TWEO stability result, the SFT-pruning comparison, the long-CoT/instruction-following trade-off) are each backed by a specific controlled comparison rather than an unstated assumption; the decontamination and quality-grading procedures are disclosed with concrete, checkable thresholds; and the fully-open release itself, if the linked artifacts match what is described, is a genuine and verifiable contribution independent of how the benchmark comparisons are framed.

## Relevance to us

This report is a concrete, fully-documented reference point for the "physics-attuned replaceable backbone plus durable train/eval/verify pipeline" bet (B02): it demonstrates, with disclosed data recipes and released checkpoints at every training stage, that a small (7.39B) dense model trained on an efficient, reproducible recipe can reach strong math-reasoning performance among comparable-scale models by combining internal reasoning with tool use rather than relying on raw parametric memorization, which is directly the same design principle (verification and search compensating for a smaller backbone's limited memorized knowledge) that motivates several of our own approved bets around physics-attuned backbones and intervention-capable world models. The report's specific efficiency techniques (gated hybrid SWA, FP8-with-TWEO, Muon, MDP-style dense mid-training supervision for agentic traces) are each independently transferable engineering choices worth tracking regardless of the paper's physics irrelevance, since a domain-specialized backbone under our own compute constraints faces the same small-model-versus-frontier-scale tension this report addresses directly, though as a systems/training-recipe report it says nothing about physics reasoning specifically and its "AI4AI" autonomy findings, while an interesting internal data point, are self-reported team judgment rather than an externally validated capability measurement.
