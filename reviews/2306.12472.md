## What this paper is about

Quantum field theories (QFTs) can look completely different on the surface — different gauge groups, different fields — while secretly describing the exact same physics; this is called a duality. String theory offers a geometric way to find and understand such dualities: build the QFT as the low-energy theory living on branes (extended objects) sitting at a singular point in some extra-dimensional geometry, and let properties of that geometry reveal properties of the QFT that might not be obvious from the field-theory side alone. This dissertation, combining three research projects, uses this "brane engineering" approach to do three things: (1) construct a new class of geometric backgrounds ("Spin(7) orientifolds") that realize a family of two-dimensional gauge theories with a small amount of supersymmetry; (2) show that these same backgrounds explain and generalize a known three-way duality ("triality") among such theories; and (3) give a unified, top-down geometric construction of "non-invertible" duality symmetry defects — a recently understood and actively studied kind of generalized symmetry — in four-dimensional gauge theories, by wrapping stringy objects called 7-branes around the geometry "at infinity."

## Extended summary

The dissertation's throughline is D-branes probing (sitting at) a singular point of a non-compact extra-dimensional geometry, so that the low-energy physics on the branes' worldvolume is a QFT decoupled from gravity, with its properties determined by the geometry of the singularity. The Introduction reviews the two organizing themes used throughout: dualities arising from the non-uniqueness of the brane-to-gauge-theory map (the same singular geometry can give rise to more than one gauge-theory description, so those descriptions must be dual to each other — the geometric analogue of Seiberg duality is given as a worked example, using D3-branes probing the complex cone over the zeroth Hirzebruch surface), and generalized (including non-invertible) global symmetries, understood via topological symmetry operators that can be constructed geometrically from "branes at infinity" whose worldvolume supports a topological field theory (TFT) governing the symmetry's fusion rules.

Chapter 2 introduces Spin(7) orientifolds: a new class of backgrounds built by taking a Calabi-Yau four-fold (CY4) cone probed by D1-branes (which ordinarily gives a 2d N=(0,2) gauge theory) and quotienting it by a combination of an anti-holomorphic involution and worldsheet parity. The anti-holomorphic involution alone turns the CY4 cone into a Spin(7) cone (a manifold with reduced, Spin(7) holonomy), which is precisely Joyce's known mathematical construction of Spin(7) manifolds from Calabi-Yau four-folds; combining this with the orientifold (worldsheet-parity) quotient yields a 2d N=(0,1) gauge theory that is explicitly realized as a "real slice" of the parent N=(0,2) theory. The construction is illustrated on both orbifold singularities (e.g., C^4 and its Z2 and Z2×Z2 quotients) and non-orbifold examples, the role of the choice of "vector structure" in the orientifold quotient is discussed, and partial resolutions of the singularity are examined; anomaly cancellation is used throughout as the mechanism that fixes otherwise-undetermined gauge-group ranks in the resulting quiver gauge theories. The chapter's own stated limitation is that the map from a given Spin(7) orientifold to its 2d N=(0,1) gauge theory is one-to-many rather than one-to-one, a gap the dissertation explicitly says is picked up in the next chapter.

Chapter 3 shows that this one-to-many map is exactly the geometric origin of a recently proposed 2d N=(0,1) triality (a three-way duality analogous to, but distinct from, an established 2d N=(0,2) triality that itself arises because multiple gauge theories can correspond to the same CY4). Specifically, Spin(7) orientifolds built using a particular involution the dissertation calls the "universal involution" reproduce exactly the previously known N=(0,1) triality. The chapter's central further claim is that this is only a special case: general Spin(7) orientifolds (not restricted to the universal involution) extend triality to a richer family of theories that mix coupled N=(0,2) and N=(0,1) sectors, interpolating between the two pure cases, and the geometric construction gives a concrete, general prescription for how flavor fields transform under this extended triality (worked out for several explicit example geometries, including Q^{1,1,1}/Z2, with the resulting web of dual "phases" traced out).

Chapter 4 turns to four dimensions and non-invertible duality/triality defects — topological symmetry operators associated with special points in a QFT's parameter space where a would-be duality maps the theory back to itself. The setup is D3-branes probing an isolated Calabi-Yau threefold cone singularity X (with C^3 itself giving 4d N=4 super Yang-Mills as the maximally supersymmetric case, and quotients C^3/Γ giving N=1 quiver gauge theories at points of enhanced, tuned coupling). The type IIB string theory duality group SL(2,Z) descends to a duality of the 4d worldvolume theory, and the dissertation's central proposal is that the corresponding topological duality/triality defects are implemented by (p,q) 7-branes wrapped on the asymptotic boundary of X "at infinity." Dimensionally reducing the relevant IIB topological term on that boundary produces a 5d "symmetry TFT" governing the 4d theory's anomalies and generalized symmetries; the wrapped 7-branes become codimension-two objects in this 5d picture, carrying branch cuts, and the dissertation's key finding is that the choice of where a branch cut is allowed to terminate reproduces, as specific special cases, two previously separately-constructed field-theory mechanisms for building duality defects (gauging a 1-form symmetry in the presence of a mixed anomaly, and "half-space gauging"), with "hybrid" branch-cut configurations giving further constructions — offering a single, top-down, geometric picture that unifies these previously distinct bottom-up recipes, for both the maximally supersymmetric case and a class of less-supersymmetric N=1 quiver theories. The chapter explicitly restricts to isolated singularities, stating this is "more to obtain some technical simplifications" than a fundamental limitation of the approach, and lists extending to non-isolated singularities and to other, non-D3-brane top-down constructions as future directions.

## Learnings

A reduction in a theory's supersymmetry can sometimes be engineered as a purely geometric quotient rather than as an independent construction: Spin(7) orientifolds realize the less-supersymmetric 2d N=(0,1) theory as a "real slice" of the more-supersymmetric N=(0,2) theory living on the same brane probe, by quotienting the underlying Calabi-Yau four-fold with an anti-holomorphic involution (which reduces the holonomy to Spin(7), following Joyce's known mathematical construction) combined with worldsheet parity. This is a transferable pattern for relating theories with different amounts of symmetry: find the geometric group action whose quotient realizes the symmetry reduction, rather than building the reduced theory from scratch.

A field-theory duality does not need to be independently discovered in field theory before a geometric construction can produce it: if a fixed singular geometry probed by branes admits more than one distinct low-energy gauge-theory description, those descriptions must be physically equivalent (dual) simply because they describe the same brane configuration, and the multiplicity of the geometry-to-gauge-theory map is itself evidence for, and in this dissertation a source of, dualities that might not otherwise have been anticipated from the field-theory side (the N=(0,1) triality's geometric origin in Chapter 3, and the extended, richer triality that follows from considering more general orientifold involutions than the one already known to reproduce the established result).

Making one previously-implicit structural choice explicit can reveal that superficially different constructions are the same idea in disguise: this dissertation's account of duality defects shows that two previously separately-derived field-theory recipes for building a topological duality defect (gauging a 1-form symmetry with a mixed 't Hooft anomaly, versus half-space gauging) both arise from wrapping the same kind of object (a 7-brane) on the geometry "at infinity," differing only in where the resulting branch cut in the 5d symmetry TFT is allowed to terminate. Naming and varying that one free choice unifies what looked like two different bottom-up tricks into one top-down picture with a shared origin.

## Verification

The dissertation's primary internal-consistency check throughout is 't Hooft anomaly cancellation: whenever a candidate gauge theory is proposed as the worldvolume theory of a brane configuration (or as one member of a proposed dual pair), the ranks of otherwise-undetermined gauge group factors are fixed by requiring that gauge anomalies cancel, which both derives specific quantitative content (e.g., the rank of the central node in the Chapter 3 triality-dual pair is stated to be fixed this way) and serves as a nontrivial consistency requirement any incorrect proposed duality would very likely fail. A second, structurally different form of verification recurs across all three chapters: reproducing an already-established special case as a consistency check on a more general new construction — Chapter 3's "universal involution" is explicitly shown to reduce to the previously known N=(0,1) triality result before the dissertation argues the more general construction extends it further, and Chapter 4's general 7-brane/branch-cut proposal is checked against, and shown to reproduce, two independently-derived bottom-up field-theory constructions of duality defects (gauging with a mixed anomaly; half-space gauging) as specific choices of branch-cut termination, plus the well-established N=4 super Yang-Mills case as the C^3 special case. Neither anomaly matching nor case-reduction is a numerical or experimental test; both are standard, deterministic consistency requirements within formal quantum field theory and string theory, which is the correct and expected standard of evidence for this kind of top-down constructive work, distinct from (and not directly comparable to) the statistical or benchmark-based verification appropriate to empirical machine-learning claims.

## Field context

The dissertation builds directly on, and explicitly cites as its point of departure, Seiberg duality for 4d N=1 gauge theories and its known geometric realization via D3-branes probing Calabi-Yau three-folds (including the Klebanov-Witten theory on the conifold), and on the general framework of understanding generalized global symmetries via topological operators (attributed to the by-now standard reference on generalized symmetries) together with its brane-theoretic realization via "branes at infinity" from recent prior work by some of the same collaborators. Chapter 2-3's results extend a previously established 2d N=(0,2) triality (arising because multiple gauge theories can correspond to the same Calabi-Yau four-fold) and a previously proposed 2d N=(0,1) triality, both cited as specific prior results the dissertation's more general Spin(7)-orientifold construction is shown to reproduce and extend. Chapter 4 sits within the recent and active literature on non-invertible and duality/triality defects (citing a broad list of contributions on this topic, plus a recent review), and on the "symmetry topological field theory" (symmetry TFT) formalism used to organize a QFT's generalized symmetries and anomalies via a bulk topological theory in one higher dimension. None of these specific precedents, nor the three papers this dissertation compiles, are yet reviewed elsewhere in this corpus, so this field positioning reflects the dissertation's own framing rather than a cross-check against another review here; this is also, more generally, the first pure theoretical-physics research contribution (as opposed to an AI/ML paper about or adjacent to physics) reviewed in this corpus.

## Critical discussion

The appropriate standard for a constructive, top-down theoretical-physics dissertation of this kind is internal mathematical and physical consistency together with honest scoping of what is and is not established, not the empirical-evaluation standard (baselines, statistical power, held-out test sets) appropriate to a machine-learning paper, and by that standard this work holds up well. Each chapter grounds its central proposal in a recognized consistency requirement (anomaly cancellation) and in reproducing an already-established special case before claiming a more general extension, which is the right order of operations: show the new construction contains the known result, then argue it goes beyond it. The dissertation is also explicit, rather than silent, about the boundaries of what it has shown: Chapter 2 states plainly that the orientifold-to-gauge-theory correspondence is one-to-many and not yet a settled bijection (motivating Chapter 3 rather than hiding the gap), and Chapter 4 states directly that its restriction to isolated Calabi-Yau singularities is a simplifying choice "rather than there being any fundamental obstacle," naming the non-isolated case as an open, not yet attempted, extension.

Two more genre-specific caveats are worth naming precisely, both already acknowledged by the dissertation itself rather than hidden. First, the branch-cut proposal in Chapter 4 is checked against known bottom-up constructions primarily through matching final answers (the resulting duality-defect mechanisms) rather than through an independent derivation of the branch-cut termination rule from first principles; the dissertation's own Appendix explicitly flags this as requiring "further checks," for instance via dimensional reduction on other internal manifolds, as an open task rather than a completed one. Second, because brane engineering results of this kind are formal, non-perturbative arguments about worldvolume theories and topological terms, they are not and cannot straightforwardly be checked against numerical lattice results or experiment; the field's accepted standard of evidence is consistency (anomaly matching, recovering known limits, cross-checking against independently derived bottom-up constructions), and this dissertation meets that standard on its own terms without claiming a stronger form of verification than the subfield provides.

What remains supported, on the dissertation's own stated terms: Spin(7) orientifolds are a well-defined, explicitly worked-out geometric construction realizing 2d N=(0,1) gauge theories as real slices of N=(0,2) ones, verified through multiple explicit orbifold and non-orbifold examples and anomaly-based rank determination; the "universal involution" case is shown, not merely asserted, to reduce to the previously known N=(0,1) triality; and the Chapter 4 branch-cut proposal is shown to reproduce two independently known duality-defect constructions as special cases, which is real, checkable evidence for the claimed unification, even though the dissertation itself flags the underlying branch-cut-termination rule as wanting further first-principles justification.
