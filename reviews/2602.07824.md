Citation: Qin, Y., Huang, Z., Mi, T., Si, W., Zhou, C., Guo, Q., Feng, S., Liu, P. Data Darwinism Part I: Unlocking the Value of Scientific Data for Pre-training. arXiv:2602.07824, 2026.

## What this paper is about

Scientific literature (papers, textbooks) is dense with knowledge, but almost no open-source pre-training corpus uses it seriously, and this paper asks why and what it takes to fix it. The core empirical finding is that just scraping and lightly filtering scientific text and adding it to a pre-training mix barely helps at all — the authors call this the "learnability gap": scientific writing assumes background knowledge, skips reasoning steps, and is written expert-to-expert, so a language model gets very little useful signal from raw exposure to it despite the text's high information density. The paper's fix is to have large language models actively rewrite the corpus — first cleaning it up, then explicitly spelling out the reasoning steps, jargon, and intuitions that expert authors leave implicit — and it shows, via a genuinely large-scale controlled pre-training experiment (models trained entirely from scratch specifically to have zero prior scientific exposure), that this rewritten version produces real, measurable model improvements where the raw version did not.

## Extended summary

The paper proposes "Data Darwinism," a ten-level taxonomy (L0-L9) for categorizing data-processing operations by how much they transform the underlying data and how much of the transformation is human- vs. model-driven: L0 (raw acquisition) through L3 (lightweight-model filtering) only ever select or discard content; L4 (Generative Refinement) and L5 (Cognitive Completion) begin to actively rewrite content while preserving semantic fidelity; L6-L9 (not implemented here) progress toward linking in external context, synthesizing executable environments, multi-agent ecosystems, and ultimately full simulated worlds. The paper implements and empirically validates only L0-L5.

Darwin-Science, the resulting corpus, is built from scanned academic books/papers plus arXiv/PubMed Central/S2ORC subsets of TxT360, OCR'd with olmOCR-7B (L1), deduplicated via MinHash and rule-filtered for size/garbling/language (L2, removing 22% via dedup alone), then classified by a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-0.5B model (EAI-Distill-0.5B) into 9 disciplines and into book-vs-paper categories, with non-educational content dropped (L3). L4 (Generative Refinement) uses GPT-OSS-120B, chunked at ~256-token windows, to delete non-academic noise (references, headers, OCR garbage) and repair formatting (merged fragmented text, restored equations/tables) while explicitly constrained to add no external knowledge — this step alone removes 20% of remaining corpus volume. L5 (Cognitive Completion), applied only to papers (not books, for cost reasons) using Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct on 1,024-token windows, does three things: reasoning reconstruction (expanding logical leaps like "it follows that" into explicit derivations), terminological explication (defining jargon inline), and pedagogical bridging (grounding abstractions in concrete analogies). The final corpus is 900B tokens (50M documents: 251.5B tokens of L4-only book data, 215B tokens of L4-only paper data, and 440B tokens of L5-processed paper data), decontaminated against GSM8K/MATH/MMLU via exact 20-gram matching (removing ~0.03% of data). A public 496B-token subset is released.

To evaluate domain-specific comprehension beyond what standard benchmarks capture, the paper builds Darwin-Science-Eval: Qwen3-32B (in thinking mode) is prompted to identify knowledge-worthy 4096-token chunks from held-out documents and generate seven-option multiple-choice questions grounded strictly in the source text (the model is instructed not to draw on its own knowledge); a two-stage LLM filter (again Qwen3-32B) then checks question self-containment (independence from external context) and answer correctness against the source text. This yields 150K questions (140K from books, 10K from papers), with 1,500-question subsamples (BookQA/PaperQA) used for efficient tracking during pre-training.

The central controlled experiment: daVinci-origin-3B and -7B are pre-trained entirely from scratch on a 5.37T-token corpus (80.2% Common Crawl/Nemotron-CC, 11.2% code, 8.5% math) deliberately excluding all scientific content, producing contamination-free "clean-room" base checkpoints. From a 930B-token checkpoint, 600B tokens of continued pre-training (CPT) are run under two conditions: Baseline (same general mixture) vs. Sci-Mix (50% Darwin-Science + 50% baseline mixture, at a 1:2 book:paper ratio), with results averaged over the final 5 checkpoints (520B-600B tokens). Headline results (Table 4): Sci-Mix beats Baseline by +2.12 points (3B) and +2.95 points (7B) averaged across 20+ standard benchmarks, and by +5.60/+8.40 points on the domain-aligned Darwin-Science-Eval/BookQA/PaperQA suite specifically — described as "3× larger" gains on aligned evaluation and attributed to standard benchmarks (standardized-test style) being a distribution mismatch for content trained on research-level material. Gains are not uniform across every individual benchmark, however: several specific benchmarks show small negative deltas even as the aggregate average is positive (e.g., MATH -0.20/-0.24, ARC-Challenge -0.31 at 7B, OpenBookQA -0.84 at 3B, GPQA -1.38 at 7B, SciBench -0.12 at 3B, PIQA -0.08 at 7B), meaning the reported average gain is not a uniform improvement across every task.

Ablation experiments isolate each hierarchy level's contribution: raw scientific data (L0-L3) yields negligible gain over Baseline even on the domain-aligned eval (the "learnability gap" claim); L4 alone contributes +0.38 cumulative points; L4+L5 together contribute +1.36. Gains grow rather than saturate across the 600B-token CPT window. Larger models benefit more (7B: +2.95 vs. 3B: +2.12). A data-composition sweep finds an inverted-U on standard benchmarks peaking at 50% scientific-content ratio (pure scientific training underperforms the balanced mix), while aligned-benchmark performance rises monotonically with scientific ratio — the paper's interpretation is that "saturation" on standard metrics reflects target mismatch, not an inherent data ceiling. Book:paper ratio is found to matter little across a wide range (100:0 to 0:100), with the paper adopting 1:2 to match its available data pool. A teacher-model-quality comparison for L5 finds Qwen3-235B outperforms GPT-OSS-120B by +0.52, and L5 (either teacher) outperforms L4-only refinement by +0.75-1.27, isolating L5's incremental value beyond format cleanup. Context-length (4K vs. 32K RoPE base) shows the 32K model trailing early but overtaking by +0.80 points after sufficient adaptation. Training-stage consistency checks (starting CPT from an early 930B-token checkpoint vs. a later 4T-token checkpoint) find comparable relative gains (+0.98 vs. +0.76), which the paper interprets as validating early-checkpoint evaluation as a cheaper proxy for corpus quality assessment.

## Learnings

The most transferable finding is the negative result itself: that raw or lightly-filtered high-information-density text (L0-L3 processing) can provide negligible pre-training value despite genuinely containing dense, non-redundant information, because the text's implicit reasoning structure and assumed background knowledge make it opaque to the model as training signal. This decouples "information density" from "learnability" as two separate properties of a corpus, which is a useful conceptual distinction for anyone building domain-specific pre-training data beyond just scientific text (e.g., legal, financial, or other expert-register domains that share the same expert-to-expert writing style).

A second learning is the specific characterization of what closes that gap: not more filtering or more volume, but active LLM-driven rewriting that explicitly reconstructs the reasoning steps an expert author skipped, defines jargon inline rather than assuming it, and grounds abstractions in concrete analogies (L5's three named sub-operations). This gives a concrete, reusable recipe (reasoning reconstruction, terminological explication, pedagogical bridging) rather than a vague prescription to "improve data quality," and the ablation isolating L5's contribution beyond L4 (format cleanup) specifically demonstrates the reasoning-explication step is doing distinct work, not just further denoising.

A third, more methodological learning is the paper's approach to controlling for checkpoint-artifact confounds in domain-specific continued-pretraining research: training fully from-scratch, contamination-free base models specifically excluding the domain under study, so that any measured gain can be unambiguously attributed to the data intervention rather than to an off-the-shelf checkpoint's unknown prior exposure. This is a substantial and costly methodological commitment (training two base models from scratch on 5.37T tokens) that few papers in this space undertake, and it directly addresses a common criticism of continued-pretraining ablations that reuse pre-existing checkpoints of unknown provenance.

## Verification

The paper's most costly and effective verification device is architectural: building contamination-free daVinci-origin base models from scratch specifically to eliminate a major confound (unknown prior scientific exposure in an off-the-shelf checkpoint) that would otherwise make attribution of the Sci-Mix gain to the data processing itself ambiguous. This is a real, substantive verification investment rather than a cheap disclaimer. Decontamination against downstream benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, MMLU via exact 20-gram matching) is also performed, though only against three specific benchmarks rather than the full 20+ benchmark suite used for evaluation, leaving contamination risk against other evaluated benchmarks (e.g., GPQA, SuperGPQA, MedQA) unaddressed by the described procedure. The paper's weakest point of verification concerns its own most eye-catching number: the domain-aligned Darwin-Science-Eval benchmark (source of the headline +5.60/+8.40-point, "3× larger" gains) is generated entirely by Qwen3-32B (question authoring) and filtered entirely by the same Qwen3-32B model (completeness and correctness checks), with no reported independent human validation of the benchmark's actual question quality, answer correctness rate, or susceptibility to shortcuts — in contrast to, for example, 2606.04075 in this batch, which validates its own LLM-judge pipeline against independent human annotators and reports the resulting agreement level explicitly. A benchmark generated and self-filtered by one model family, then used to evaluate models continually pre-trained on text partly rewritten by a similar family of LLMs (GPT-OSS-120B, Qwen3-235B), carries a same-family-judge risk that is not addressed here: it is plausible, though untested by the paper, that LLM-generated evaluation questions built from held-out documents could be more answerable by a model whose training corpus has been passed through similar LLM-based rewriting, independent of whether the model has genuinely acquired the underlying scientific understanding the benchmark intends to measure.

## Field context

The paper positions Darwin-Science against a landscape it characterizes as heavily mathematics-focused (OpenWebMath, MathPile, InfiMM-WebMath-40B, MegaMath, MathCoder2) and largely post-training-oriented in the broader science space (MegaScience, cited as generating science reasoning/QA from textbooks) rather than addressing foundational pre-training corpus construction for the full range of STEM disciplines beyond mathematics — the paper's claimed gap is the absence of an open, richly-parsed, multi-discipline corpus of research-grade scientific material suitable for early-stage (pre-training/mid-training) model development specifically. On data-processing methodology, it distinguishes its full hierarchy from prior individual pipeline stages: ProX and RefineX (small-model-executed fine-grained edit programs for corpus surgery), Nemotron-CC (classifier ensembles plus synthetic rephrasing), and WRAP (web-text-to-QA/Wikipedia-style rephrasing) are each credited with advancing one stage of what this paper frames as a broader, unified hierarchy, with the explicit claim that no prior system had combined comprehensive scientific-literature collection, multi-stage LLM-based semantic cleaning, and pedagogical (reasoning-explicating) rewriting in one pipeline. Within this corpus, the paper's "learnability gap" finding — that raw domain-dense text can be nearly worthless for pre-training regardless of information content, and that the fix is explicit reasoning-reconstruction rather than more volume or lighter filtering — is conceptually adjacent to but empirically distinct from this batch's cross-cutting "generation outpaces verification" theme (2605.18661, 2605.20025, 2606.04075): where those papers document systems producing plausible-but-unverified outputs, this paper documents the reverse-shaped problem for pre-training data specifically — genuinely correct, information-dense content that is nonetheless unusable as a training signal until a model actively reconstructs the reasoning path connecting it to what a learner would need to see.

## Critical discussion

The paper's controlled design (from-scratch contamination-free baselines, matched CPT token budgets, systematic per-level ablation) is a genuine methodological strength relative to typical continued-pretraining ablation studies, and the L0-L3-vs-L4-vs-L5 staged comparison directly demonstrates that the reported gains come from the specific processing levels claimed rather than from an unspecified overall "more processing is better" effect. That said, several aspects of the reported numbers warrant caution. First, essentially every headline delta in the paper (Table 4's main results, the composition-ratio sweep, the processing-strategy comparison, the context-length and training-stage-consistency ablations) appears to reflect a single training run per configuration rather than multiple seeds; the paper's variance-reduction technique (averaging the final 5 checkpoints over a 600B-token window and smoothing with a moving average) reduces noise from a single trajectory's late-training fluctuation but does not substitute for repeated-seed variance, so single-decimal-point comparisons (e.g., L4's "+0.38" vs. the full hierarchy's "+1.36," or the "+0.52" teacher-model-quality gap) should be read as point estimates from one run each rather than statistically established differences. Second, the paper's own Table 4 shows the average positive deltas are not uniform: several individual benchmarks (MATH, GPQA, SciBench, OpenBookQA, ARC-Challenge, PIQA) show small negative deltas for at least one model scale even as the headline averages are positive, a detail the main text does not discuss despite it bearing directly on how broadly the "Sci-Mix outperforms Baseline" claim should be read — the aggregate framing in the abstract and Finding 2/3 narrative could leave a reader with a stronger impression of uniform improvement than the underlying table actually supports.

Third, and most consequential for the paper's most attention-grabbing specific claim (the "3× larger," domain-aligned +5.60/+8.40-point gains), the Darwin-Science-Eval benchmark that produces this number is entirely LLM-generated and LLM-filtered (both by Qwen3-32B) with no reported independent human check on question validity or correctness rate, unlike the paper's own careful contamination-avoidance work on the training side. Given that this benchmark is also the paper's primary evidence for the claim that "aligned evaluation reveals hidden gains" that standard benchmarks miss, a reader should weigh this specific number with somewhat more caution than the paper's confident framing suggests, pending an independent validation of the benchmark's own construction quality (e.g., a human-annotator agreement study analogous to the one 2606.04075 in this batch conducts for its own LLM-judge pipeline).

What remains well supported after these caveats: the central qualitative claim — that raw scientific text is a poor pre-training signal regardless of its information density, and that LLM-driven reasoning-explication (not merely cleanup or added volume) is necessary to unlock its value — is demonstrated through a genuinely controlled, staged ablation (L0-L3 vs. L4 vs. L5) on contamination-free from-scratch base models, which is a stronger evidentiary basis than most domain-pretraining papers provide for an analogous claim. The finding that data-composition optimum is goal-dependent (50% for general-purpose balance; monotonically increasing for domain-aligned performance) is a specific, actionable, and internally consistent result rather than a single point estimate, since it is demonstrated across a full ratio sweep (15%-100%) with a clear and interpretable inverted-U vs. monotonic contrast between the two evaluation targets.
