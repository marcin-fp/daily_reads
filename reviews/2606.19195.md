Citation: Duan, K., Xu, Z., Liu, W., Ruan, X., Chen, X., Wang, X. Moebius: 0.2B Lightweight Image Inpainting Framework with 10B-Level Performance. arXiv preprint, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19195v1

## What this paper is about

Image inpainting (filling in missing or masked regions of a photo) has recently been dominated by huge diffusion models like FLUX.1-Fill-Dev (11.9B parameters), which are too expensive to run on everyday hardware. This paper asks whether a much smaller model, purpose-built and trained just for inpainting rather than general image generation, can match that quality at a fraction of the size. The answer is Moebius, a 0.226B-parameter (about 2% the size) diffusion model that combines a new efficient attention-like building block with a distillation recipe operating entirely in the model's compressed latent space, reaching latency more than 15x faster in total inference time while matching or beating the giant model on several benchmarks.

## Extended summary

Moebius starts from PixelHacker (862M parameters), an existing efficient inpainting model that pairs a Latent Diffusion Model backbone with "Latent Categories Guidance" (LCG, semantic-category embeddings injected via cross-attention) and Gated Linear Attention (GLA) for efficient self-attention. The paper first shows that naively substituting further lightweight operators (depthwise convolutions, linear attention variants) into this already-efficient backbone causes a "severe representation bottleneck": FID degrades from 32.75 to 43.58 in a controlled comparison (Table 2), and GLA specifically cannot perform cross-attention at all, which blocks it from handling the LCG conditioning that PixelHacker relies on.

The proposed fix is the Local-lambda Mix Interaction (LlambdaMI) block, built around two new modules. Local-lambda replaces quadratic self-attention with a bilinear form: instead of computing full attention maps, it summarizes local spatial context and semantic content into two fixed-size linear matrices (a content mapping and a positional mapping), and the query interacts with these compact summaries directly, giving linear complexity. Interactive-lambda does the analogous thing for cross-attention against the external LCG semantic-prior embedding, adding a lightweight positional embedding to handle the scale mismatch between the small LCG embedding grid and the larger latent spatial grid, which the paper argues is what lets a GLA-incompatible operation (cross-attention with an external condition) be performed efficiently for the first time in this efficient-attention family. These are combined with depthwise residual blocks (replacing standard convolutions) and Mix-FFN (replacing standard feed-forward layers) to form the final LlambdaMI block, yielding a 226M-parameter, 0.154 TFLOP backbone (down from 862M/PixelHacker's larger footprint).

To recover the representational capacity lost to this compression, the paper introduces an adaptive multi-granularity distillation strategy performed entirely within the latent space (avoiding the memory cost of decoding to pixel space during training). It combines a coarse-grained bottleneck-alignment loss (matching an intermediate latent between teacher and student), a fine-grained final-output alignment loss plus the standard noise-prediction task loss, and a latent-space perceptual loss (E-LatentLPIPS) that provides perceptual supervision without pixel decoding. Because these losses differ greatly in scale and gradient contribution, the paper computes adaptive weights from each loss's gradient norm with respect to relevant parameter subsets (both within the fine-grained losses and across coarse/fine granularities) rather than using fixed hyperparameter weights.

The main results compare Moebius against academic specialists (LaMa, MAT, MI-GAN, and others) and industrial generalists (FLUX.1-Fill-Dev, SD3.5-Large-Inpainting) on Places2 (natural scenes, four mask/resolution configurations), CelebA-HQ and FFHQ (portraits), plus out-of-distribution sets (LVIS, DeepFakeFace) and a 22-participant double-blind user study. On portrait benchmarks, Moebius clearly and consistently outperforms FLUX.1-Fill-Dev (5.39 vs. 10.13 FID on CelebA-HQ, 8.15 vs. 11.19 FID on FFHQ) and is close to its own teacher PixelHacker. On natural-scene Places2, the picture is more mixed across the four tested mask/resolution configurations: Moebius wins clearly on Places2 (Small) (0.92 vs. 0.94 FID, the split highlighted in the abstract and the paper's headline summary table), splits with FLUX on Test and Large (better LPIPS but worse FID than FLUX on both), and loses clearly to FLUX on Places2 (256) (14.13 vs. 10.44 FID, a 26% gap, and 0.470 vs. 0.391 LPIPS, a 17% gap). The paper's headline "breaking the impossible triangle" table (Table 1) reports only the Places2 (Small) configuration among the four available Places2 splits alongside CelebA-HQ and FFHQ. Efficiency is measured under an explicitly standardized protocol (single L40S GPU, batch size 1, 512x512): Moebius reaches 26.01 ms/step versus 161.01 ms/step for FLUX.1-Fill-Dev (a single-step speedup), and factoring in each model's own default number of sampling steps (20 for Moebius vs. 50 for FLUX, 28 for SD3.5) yields the paper's reported >15x total-inference-time speedup. The user study (50 cases per scenario, 22 participants, forced-choice) finds Moebius (31.76% average preference) statistically close to its teacher PixelHacker (32.18%) and clearly ahead of FLUX.1-Fill-Dev (23.70%) and SD3.5 (12.36%), with no confidence intervals or significance test reported. Supplementary material adds qualitative comparisons against commercial editing systems (Nano Banana, Qwen Image Edit), a documented failure mode (Moebius loses some fine detail versus its teacher in tiny, low-context background regions), a classifier-free-guidance scale ablation, and out-of-distribution evaluation on LVIS and DeepFakeFace, on which Moebius remains competitive with FLUX and PixelHacker. Ablation tables systematically vary attention mechanism, spatial block type, and FFN type across 15 configurations (Table 2) and isolate the incremental contribution of each distillation loss term (Table 5), showing that architecture-only compression without the proposed distillation plateaus at a markedly worse FID (33.42) than the full recipe (26.43). ## Learnings

The "summarize into fixed-size linear matrices" technique underlying Local-lambda and Interactive-lambda — replacing a quadratic attention map with a bilinear query-times-compact-summary-matrix formulation, where the compact matrix aggregates either local spatial context or an external semantic-prior embedding — is a specific, transferable technique for building a linear-complexity attention-equivalent that can still condition on an external embedding via a cross-attention-like operation, addressing a concrete limitation the paper identifies in existing efficient-attention variants (Gated Linear Attention) that structurally cannot perform cross-attention at all.

The ablation-demonstrated finding that naive lightweight-operator substitution catastrophically degrades a compressed diffusion backbone on its own (FID 32.75 to 43.58 in Table 2's Exp1-to-Exp2 comparison), while the same substitutions paired with a co-designed distillation strategy recover most of the lost quality (Exp6-to-Exp9's progression to FID 26.43 with the full compressed architecture), is a transferable methodological lesson: for extreme model compression, the architectural compression and the training/distillation objective need to be co-designed together rather than treated as independently optimizable stages, since architecture-only compression plateaus well above what architecture-plus-distillation together achieve (Table 2, Exp10: FID 33.42 without distillation on the final compressed architecture, versus 26.43 with it).

Performing knowledge distillation, including the perceptual-loss term, entirely within a diffusion model's latent space (via a memory-efficient latent-space LPIPS variant) rather than decoding to pixel space for perceptual supervision is a specific, reusable efficiency technique directly transferable to any latent-diffusion distillation pipeline seeking perceptual-quality supervision without the memory and compute cost of full VAE decoding at every training step.

## Verification

The paper's ablation methodology is genuinely thorough: Table 2 systematically varies three architectural axes (attention mechanism, spatial block type, FFN type) crossed with distillation on/off across 15 configurations, letting a reader attribute the final design's quality to specific component choices rather than treating it as a monolithic result; Table 5 separately isolates each distillation loss term's incremental contribution. Efficiency comparisons are measured under an explicitly standardized protocol (single L40S GPU, batch size 1, 512x512) rather than compiled from disparate sources with different hardware, which is a real methodological strength for a paper making efficiency claims. The main gap is in how the headline results are selected and aggregated rather than in how they were measured: the primary summary table (Table 1) reports only the Places2 (Small) mask configuration among the four Places2 splits evaluated in the full results table (Table 3), and the full breakdown shows Moebius performing worse than FLUX.1-Fill-Dev in FID on three of those four configurations (splitting on LPIPS for Test and Large, and losing clearly on both metrics for Places2 (256), where FLUX is 26% better in FID and 17% better in LPIPS). This is a genuinely mixed natural-scene result that the headline table's single-split selection does not convey, in contrast to the portrait-domain (CelebA-HQ, FFHQ) results, which are consistent, substantial wins against FLUX.1-Fill-Dev across the board and are not similarly selectively reported. The user study, while methodologically sound (double-blind, forced-choice, 22 participants), reports no confidence intervals or significance test, leaving the robustness of its reported near-tie between Moebius and its own teacher PixelHacker (31.76% vs. 32.18%) unestablished.

## Field context

Moebius sits in the efficient/lightweight diffusion architecture literature it cites (depthwise convolutions, group convolutions, low-rank FFN designs, linear-attention mechanisms including Gated Linear Attention) and diffusion-model knowledge distillation, explicitly distinguishing itself from the dominant diffusion-KD focus on timestep/sampling-step distillation (accelerating denoising with fewer steps) by instead targeting architectural-capacity transfer for extreme parameter compression. Its direct point of departure is PixelHacker, the prior SOTA efficient inpainting model (Latent Categories Guidance plus Gated Linear Attention) that Moebius uses as its teacher and compresses roughly 4x further while narrowing but not closing the quality gap on natural scenes and closing or exceeding it on portrait scenes. This paper sits outside the dominant LLM/agent theme of the surrounding batch, and no direct connections to other reviews in this corpus were identified; the general principle it demonstrates — that architectural compression and distillation strategy must be co-designed rather than optimized independently to avoid a representation-collapse bottleneck — is a domain-general lesson for model compression that parallels (without being directly comparable to) similar co-design arguments seen in efficiency-mechanism papers elsewhere in this batch, such as the joint kernel-and-training-recipe design in MiniMax Sparse Attention.

## Critical discussion

The paper's architectural contribution is well-motivated and concretely validated: the LlambdaMI block solves a specific, identified limitation (Gated Linear Attention's inability to perform cross-attention against an external conditioning embedding) with a formulation that is both explained mathematically and shown via ablation to meaningfully outperform naive lightweight-operator substitution. The co-designed distillation strategy is similarly well-supported by an ablation that isolates each loss term's contribution and shows the full recipe substantially outperforms architecture-only compression.

The main critical concern is in how the headline comparison against the 10B-level industrial baseline is presented rather than in the underlying method. The abstract and Table 1 frame Moebius as matching or surpassing FLUX.1-Fill-Dev's generation quality, and this framing is accurate and strongly supported for portrait scenes (CelebA-HQ, FFHQ), where Moebius wins clearly and consistently. For natural scenes (Places2), however, the full per-split breakdown in Table 3 shows a genuinely mixed result: Moebius beats FLUX clearly only on the Places2 (Small) mask configuration, splits with it on Test and Large (trading a worse FID for a better LPIPS), and loses clearly on both metrics on Places2 (256), by a 26% FID and 17% LPIPS margin. The paper's headline summary table (Table 1), which is the first table a reader encounters and the one that anchors the abstract's efficiency-versus-quality claim, reports only the Places2 (Small) configuration among these four, omitting the split where the industrial baseline wins by the largest margin. The paper's prose in Section 4.2 does hedge this somewhat ("performs on par... across various mask conditions, and even surpasses it on the Places2 (Small) benchmark"), which is a more accurate characterization than Table 1's presentation alone would suggest, but a reader relying on the abstract and Table 1 without cross-referencing the full Table 3 would come away with an overstated picture of Moebius's natural-scene performance specifically relative to FLUX. Separately, the reported >15x total-inference speedup combines a real, measured per-step latency advantage with a difference in each model's own default sampling-step count (20 vs. 50/28), which the paper discloses but which means the headline multiplier is not purely an architectural-efficiency comparison at matched sampling budgets.

What remains solidly supported after these caveats: the LlambdaMI architecture and the co-designed latent-space distillation strategy together produce a genuinely well-ablated, substantial improvement over naive compression of the same teacher backbone; the portrait-domain results represent a real, consistent, and large quality advantage over a much larger industrial generalist model at roughly 2% of its parameter count; and the per-step efficiency measurement (26.01 ms vs. 161.01 ms under matched hardware and batch conditions) is a fair, well-controlled comparison. The natural-scene claim of matching or surpassing the industrial baseline should be read as configuration-specific (strongest on small-mask inpainting) rather than as a uniform win across all tested Places2 conditions.
