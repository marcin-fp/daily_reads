Citation: Zhuoyang Qian, Biao Wu, Yiran Wang, Chris D Yan, Desan Dai, Liangwei Zheng, Jin Jiang, Junsheng Zhang, and Wenhao Wang. Spark-to-Paper: End-to-End Research Paper Generation as a Composable Skill. arXiv preprint, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11924v1

## What this paper is about

The authors build a system that turns a research idea into a finished paper — literature search, running real experiments, writing, generating editable figures, and revising claims to match evidence — but instead of building it as a standalone autonomous-agent platform (as prior systems like The AI Scientist or Agent Laboratory do), they implement it as thirteen skills that run inside an existing coding assistant (Claude Code), with no separate orchestration server. The core design bet is to split model judgment (what should this argument say, is this evidence sufficient) from deterministic, checkable operations (does this citation resolve, does the LaTeX compile), and to specify what evidence a paper needs before running the experiments that would produce it.

## Extended summary

**Architecture.** Thirteen composable skills communicate only through artifacts in a shared project directory (blueprint, bibliography, section sources, figures, results facts, compiled manuscript). A lightweight orchestrator sequences eight stages: input routing (choosing Proposal Mode, where unmeasured results must stay unspecified, versus Data-Aware Mode, where quantitative claims must be grounded in supplied data), planning, citation, writing, refinement, review, figure generation, assembly, and a conditional experiment-execution stage.

**Experiment design and claim revision.** The evidence a paper needs (datasets, baselines, metrics, ablations, result-table structure) is committed to before any experiment runs — a form of in-pipeline preregistration. After execution, each claim is assigned one of five evidence labels (supported, partially-supported, unsupported, contradicted, needs-confirmation), and revision actions follow from the label (narrow the claim, run a missing experiment, remove it, or flag it for author resolution). Negative and null results are explicitly preserved rather than dropped.

**Integrity mechanisms.** Deterministic gates (template, blueprint, citation, manuscript, figure, compilation) block properties that can be checked mechanically. Self-Review handles local consistency after each edit; Adversarial Review runs multiple isolated review passes at the manuscript level, and every raised issue must quote the passage it challenges and survive three checks (is it real, is it already addressed, is it in scope) before triggering revision. The authors name and address a specific failure mode, the "Self-Refutation Loop," where the system repeatedly concludes its own experiments don't support the original hypothesis but keeps revising the same direction instead of abandoning it; they bound this to seven experiment-critique-revision cycles, after which the trajectory is recorded as a failure report and a new idea is started fresh.

**Figures.** Quantitative figures are generated deterministically from measured data (never by an image model). Explanatory/method figures start as an image-model-generated raster, then get reconstructed into editable HTML/vector form by iteratively comparing the reconstruction against the raster target.

**Evaluation.** Across eight pre-registered, externally selected research topics, the full system reports 99.5% citation validity and 96.4% figure editability, versus 91-96% citation validity and 0-3% figure editability for released papers from three prior standalone systems (AI Scientist, AI Scientist-v2, Agent Laboratory), and 81% citation validity for a single-pass LLM baseline on the same backbone. Cost/time for prior systems are taken only from their own published numbers, not re-run. An ablation (three shared topics, four configurations) shows fabrication detection on a fixed 36-probe seeded corpus rising from 14% (single-pass, no gates) to 69% (gates only) to 81% (+self-review) to 92% (+adversarial review), with adversarial review separately reaching 74% precision on 57 blinded-rater-assessed issues (3 "cannot tell" excluded from 60 sampled). A qualitative case study (Fig. 6) shows the system pushing back against two deliberately injected incorrect user expectations (that Accuracy is the right metric for an imbalanced clinical task; that a causal decomposition model should match a plain RNN) and instead reporting what the run's own experiments showed.

**Authors' own caveats.** Table 1/Figure 1's capability comparison against seven related systems is explicitly labeled "the authors' own ordinal assessment from each system's public documentation, not a measured benchmark." The showcase per-paper output statistics (Fig. 5) are labeled "self-reported by the system's maintainers, not an independent measurement; included as existence evidence, not as a benchmark result." Cross-system cost/quality comparisons in Table 3 explicitly do not normalize for backbone model, topic set, or pricing environment between systems.

## Learnings

Separating experiment planning from experiment reporting — committing to the evidence a paper needs and the table structure before any result is observed — is a concrete, reusable mechanism against a specific and common failure mode (adapting the evaluation protocol after seeing which comparison looks best). The five-label claim-admission scheme (supported/partially-supported/unsupported/contradicted/needs-confirmation) with a fixed revision action per label is a clean, auditable way to make "does the evidence match the narrative" a checkable step rather than an implicit editorial judgment.

Naming a specific failure mode (Self-Refutation Loop) and bounding it explicitly, rather than assuming iterative self-critique always converges, is a useful design pattern for any long-horizon agentic pipeline with a self-critique loop: cap the cycles, log the failed trajectory as a first-class output, and move on rather than forcing convergence.

## Verification

Central to the paper's design, and reported with more transparency than most system papers in this space: citation validity is checked via external bibliographic metadata services rather than the pipeline's own in-loop citation gate (avoiding a same-mechanism-grading-itself problem for that specific metric); the fabrication-detection ablation uses a fixed, pre-injected 36-probe corpus held constant across all four configurations, with Wilson 95% confidence intervals reported; review precision is checked against blinded human raters (60 sampled issues, 57 after exclusions), not the system's own review verdicts. Two verification gaps are notable. First, the headline capability-comparison table and positioning figure (Table 1, Fig. 1) are explicitly self-rated by the authors from each compared system's documentation, not independently measured. Second, claim-level evidence assessment inside the pipeline itself (whether a specific result semantically supports a specific claim) is explicitly stated to be "currently performed by the model," with no independent human audit of that judgment reported at the scale of the main evaluation — the paper's rigor is strongest on artifact-level and citation-level checks, weaker on the semantic claim-assessment step that is central to its own stated contribution.

## Field context

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work it explicitly maps in Table 1 and Section 2: standalone, infrastructure-heavy autonomous research agents (The AI Scientist / v2, Agent Laboratory, Kosmos/Robin, and AutoResearchClaw) and lighter skill-based writing assistants that stop short of running real experiments (GPT Researcher, Idea2Story, karpathy/autoresearch). Its stated contribution is occupying a gap between these: full end-to-end capability (real experiments, real figures) without a standing orchestration service.

This corpus already contains an independent review of one of the paper's own comparison points: AutoResearchClaw ([2605.20025.md](2605.20025.md)), reference [12] in this paper and a row in its Table 1. Our independent review characterizes AutoResearchClaw as a 23-stage standalone pipeline (not skill-based) with a verified-results registry and cross-run memory, evaluated on a self-constructed 25-topic benchmark with small-sample, uncorrected-uncertainty issues in its headline percentage gains. Spark-to-Paper's Table 1 entry for AutoResearchClaw ("End-to-end: ✓, Runs exper.: ✓, Draws figures: ✓, Editable vectors: –, No standing infra.: –") is consistent with our independent review's characterization of it as a capable but infrastructure-heavy standalone system, which supports the accuracy of this paper's positioning table for at least that one row.

One connection is worth flagging rather than treating as ordinary related work: reference [27] (Idea2Story), which appears in Table 1 and Fig. 1's qualitative positioning chart rated near the bottom on both axes, lists Zhuoyang Qian and Biao Wu — two of this paper's own (equal-contribution) authors — as co-authors. This is stated directly in the paper's own reference list rather than inferred from outside knowledge.

## Critical discussion

The paper is unusually candid about the limits of its own evidence, which makes it easier to identify exactly where the claims outrun the data. The core quantitative results — citation validity, figure editability, and the fabrication-detection ablation — rest on real, if narrow, measurement: eight pre-registered topics, external verification services for citations, blinded human raters for review precision, and a fixed seeded-probe corpus for fabrication detection. These specific numbers are credible within their stated scope.

The paper's broader positioning claim — that Spark-to-Paper occupies a uniquely favorable point between infrastructure-heavy and infrastructure-free systems — rests more heavily on Table 1 and Fig. 1, which the authors themselves label as their own subjective, documentation-based ordinal assessment of competing systems rather than a measured benchmark (`weak-baseline`: the comparison a reader is most likely to remember, the two-axis positioning chart, is not actually a benchmark result). This matters more than usual here because one of the systems rated lowest on that chart (Idea2Story) shares two authors with this paper, so the comparison is not fully independent even in its qualitative framing — worth noting explicitly rather than treating Table 1 as an arm's-length survey.

The main quantitative comparison (Table 3) explicitly does not control for backbone model, topic set, or pricing environment across systems, which the authors disclose but which still limits what the headline 99.5%-vs-91-96% citation-validity gap can support: it demonstrates that this specific pipeline, on this backbone, on these topics, beats these systems' own self-reported numbers, not that the five core mechanisms would produce the same gap under matched conditions (`weak-baseline`/`narrow-evaluation`). The Section 7.4 case study, while a genuinely interesting demonstration of the system pushing back on injected false premises, is narrated in language ("fearlessly exposes," "demonstrating its commitment to evidence-based scientific truth") that goes beyond what two hand-picked, non-blinded case studies can establish about the system's general behavior (`overclaim`). Similarly, the fabrication-detection ablation, while methodologically sound (fixed probes, held-out configurations, confidence intervals), tests only 36 probes across ten failure families from a single injection protocol — informative about relative gains from adding gates/self-review/adversarial-review, but a narrow basis for a general claim about fabrication resistance (`narrow-evaluation`).

What remains supported after these caveats: within the paper's own instrumented runs, adding deterministic gates, then self-review, then adversarial review each measurably increases fabrication detection on a fixed probe set, and the skill-based architecture does produce citations that resolve and figures that stay editable at a higher rate than the specific artifacts audited from three prior systems. The claim that this design is a generally superior point in the autonomous-research design space is not yet demonstrated to the same standard.
