Citation: Namgyu Ho, Huzama Ahmad, Woosung Koh, Se-Young Yun, Tal Schuster, and Cicero Nogueira dos Santos. Language Models Can Control Their Own Attention. arXiv preprint, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02737v1

## What this paper is about

At every decoding step, a transformer with global attention re-reads its entire KV cache even though the actual attention weight concentrates on a tiny fraction of it — for a 1M-token context, that's a memory-bandwidth cost comparable to loading the whole model's weights, at every single step. Prior fixes predict which tokens matter using a cheap proxy score computed fresh at each step, which still costs O(N) per step. This paper asks a simpler question: if the model already "knows" which parts of the context are relevant, why not just have it say so? Declarative Attention (DA) has the model declare, in its own chain-of-thought, whether it's currently surveying the whole context, focusing on one specific chunk, or reasoning locally over what it's already written — and the inference engine reads those declarations like tool calls to build a real, hardware-level KV cache mask, entirely through prompting, with no training and no auxiliary scorer.

## Extended summary

**Protocol.** The prompt splits into a fixed "scaffold" (system instruction, question, DA instructions — always attended) and a "context" region divided into ~2K-token "magic chunks," delivered as a simulated tool-call transcript (segments boundaries land on the user/assistant/tool special tokens models already track fluently from post-training, rather than on arbitrary novel delimiters). The model freely alternates between three tagged modes with no restriction on order or frequency: `<global>` (attends to all context segments — for navigating and deciding what to look at next), `<focus magic_chunks="K">` (attends only to the named segment(s) — for extracting values verbatim), and `<local>` (attends to none of the context, only its own response so far — for synthesis). A DA state machine parses these tag transitions from the model's own output stream and, integrated into vLLM via hooks on the attention-metadata builder (no kernel or scheduler changes), rewrites the KV-cache block table at each decode step so the attention kernel simply reads fewer blocks — inheriting FlashAttention's efficiency, with masking rounded outward to block boundaries so nothing the model asked to see is ever dropped.

**Why this should pay off in large-batch serving.** A roofline analysis (charging each operation at its own hardware ceiling — compute-bound matmuls via Model FLOPs Utilization, memory-bound KV reads via Model Bandwidth Utilization) argues that in optimized large-batch deployment, the global-attention KV read dominates decode time and grows with context length, while FFN cost is fixed by active parameters and context-independent; DA's savings land entirely on the one term that dominates and grows.

**Main results (15 long-context tasks, 6 models across Gemma-4 and Qwen-3.5/3.6 families).** On the two largest models, DA cuts average attended tokens per response by 52.0% (Gemma-4-31B) and 31.1% (Qwen-3.6-27B), with modest average accuracy drops of 1.27pp and 2.75pp; DA matches or beats vanilla on 7/15 and 5/15 tasks respectively, and losses concentrate in multi-span reasoning tasks more than single-span retrieval. A dedicated ablation (DA-no-mask, DA-nm: same prompt and mode instructions, but full causal attention) isolates the source of both effects: the chunked prompt format alone is nearly free (DA-nm matches or nearly matches vanilla accuracy) but actually increases attended tokens (because the DA protocol induces ~15-35% more decode steps than vanilla); the mask is what converts that overhead into a net saving, cutting attended tokens by 71.1% (Gemma) and 46.5% (Qwen) relative to DA-nm, and it is also responsible for essentially all of DA's accuracy cost relative to DA-nm. Accuracy scales favorably with backbone size (from 29% relative accuracy retention at Gemma-4-E4B to 99% at Gemma-4-31B; from 64% at Qwen-3.5-4B to 97% at Qwen-3.6-27B), tracking a separately measured protocol-adherence rate (the fraction of `<focus>` calls that parse to a valid chunk reference) that rises from 58% to 99% across the same size range — directly showing that most of the smallest model's accuracy collapse is a failure to follow the protocol, not a failure to reason once information is available. Absolute token savings grow sharply with context length (about 1M tokens saved at short context, ~21M in the longest bin), while relative accuracy stays within ~1pp of vanilla up to 32K tokens before declining modestly (to ~96% relative) beyond that.

**Efficiency projection.** A roofline-estimated decode wall-clock time (not a directly measured end-to-end benchmark) projects DA reduces decode cost to 0.71x of vanilla on Gemma-4-31B and 0.77x on Qwen-3.6-27B on a single B200 GPU at stated utilization assumptions (40% MFU, 70% MBU), explicitly described by the authors as "a theoretical ceiling at the stated utilizations rather than measured wall-clock time," and excluding prefill (assumed to run on a separately disaggregated pool). The paper also separately estimates, using the same roofline method, that global attention remains 56-97% of decode time on several very recent sparse/indexer-based architectures (DeepSeek-V4-Pro/Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash, MiniMax-M3) and 94% on Kimi-K3 at 1M-token context, arguing the underlying cost DA targets persists even as newer architectures optimize the KV cache in other ways.

**Self-stated limitations (Section 8).** The authors are explicit that every limitation they identify is a property of the zero-shot elicitation, not of the masking mechanism itself: DA runs about a third more decode steps than vanilla because the zero-shot mode mix isn't optimized (a post-training pipeline could likely fix this); benchmark contexts must be artificially chunked into "magic chunks," whereas real agentic contexts already carry natural segment boundaries (tool calls, turns) that DA might exploit more directly; the protocol works only in non-thinking mode, because preliminary experiments found models fail to follow DA's tag syntax inside thinking traces; and `<global>` steps retain full attention cost and account for over 80% of DA's remaining attended tokens (rising further at longer context), which the authors suggest could be reduced by having the model navigate a compact in-context index rather than the full context.

## Learnings

The core methodological move — deriving a real, enforceable hardware attention mask directly from parseable text the model already produces (rather than approximating relevance from internal activations or a separate learned scorer) — is a specific instance of a more general, transferable idea: when a system needs to act on "what the model is attending to," reading it off an explicit textual declaration, rather than inferring it from hidden state, converts an approximation problem into a parsing problem, and can make the resulting behavior auditable by a human in the same text that governs the model's own resource use.

The DA-vs-DA-no-mask ablation design is a clean, reusable pattern for any paper introducing a prompting protocol that changes both the model's output format and some downstream mechanism (here, the attention mask): by holding the format fixed and only removing the mechanism, the paper cleanly separates "does the new prompt format itself change behavior" from "does the added mechanism do the work," rather than leaving the two effects confounded in a single before-after comparison.

The diagnosis that a smaller model's accuracy collapse under a new protocol is substantially a protocol-adherence failure rather than a reasoning failure — supported by directly measuring the syntax-parsing success rate alongside accuracy, rather than inferring the cause from accuracy alone — is a specific, reusable technique for attributing performance drops in any setting where a model must both follow a new output convention and solve the underlying task.

## Verification

Central and, in several places, unusually rigorous: the DA-no-mask ablation isolates the mask's causal contribution from the prompt format's effect; the model-scaling analysis directly measures and reports protocol-adherence (parseable focus calls) alongside accuracy, rather than treating accuracy alone as diagnostic; and the efficiency claim is explicitly and repeatedly labeled a theoretical roofline projection rather than a measured wall-clock result, with the authors stating this distinction themselves rather than blurring it. The free-form accuracy metric relies on an LLM judge (a thinking-enabled Qwen-3.5-4B, chosen from a family different from most evaluated backbones) applying a rubric generated by Gemini-3-Flash, validated by a single reported correlation check against Gemini-3.1-Pro (Pearson r=0.99) rather than against independent human ratings. Four of the fifteen evaluation sources use synthetic QA generated by Gemini-3-Flash rather than original human-authored questions, disclosed transparently in Table 1 rather than hidden in the aggregate results. What is not reported: any confidence interval, variance, or significance test for the headline accuracy or attended-token deltas, and no directly measured (as opposed to roofline-projected) end-to-end wall-clock benchmark of the custom vLLM integration that the paper otherwise describes in detail.

## Field context

DA is explicitly positioned as an orthogonal third approach alongside two existing lines of sparse-attention work it surveys in Appendix A: static heuristic KV eviction (recency- or magnitude-based, e.g. StreamingLLM-style attention sinks) and lightweight-scan dynamic scoring methods (which approximate relevance via a cheap proxy computed fresh at every step, at persistent O(N) per-step cost). It builds directly on the attention-sink finding (fixing the system instruction as a sink so context tokens never need to absorb that role) and cites a closely related trained precedent (Self-Selected Attention Span, which trains task-specific span selection within 2K-token contexts) that DA extends into a single, task-agnostic, zero-shot, ~100K-token-scale protocol. The paper's Section 8 discussion connects DA explicitly to System 2 Attention (attention selection as explicit reasoning rather than an implicit internal computation) and sketches concrete synergies with lightweight-scan methods (which could handle the `<global>` steps DA still spends full cost on), speculative decoding (whose sequential-step savings compound with DA's per-step savings), and a proposed "reversible context compaction" that would let out-of-focus KV cache spill to host memory and be prefetched exactly when a `<focus>` declaration names it, distinguishing this from destructive context-summarization approaches. No paper currently in this corpus addresses KV-cache efficiency or sparse attention specifically, so this is a novel connection point within the corpus.

## Critical discussion

The paper's own Section 8 is unusually candid for a methods paper, explicitly separating what is a property of the DA mechanism (which the ablations support directly) from what is an artifact of eliciting it zero-shot from off-the-shelf models (which the authors argue, but do not yet demonstrate, could be improved by post-training) — this kind of self-scoping is a genuine strength that pre-empts much of what a critical reader would otherwise need to independently identify. The DA-no-mask ablation and the protocol-adherence-vs-accuracy analysis are both targeted, well-designed experiments that isolate specific causal claims rather than resting on a single aggregate comparison.

Two things nonetheless bound how far the current results generalize. First, the protocol is demonstrated only in non-thinking mode, because the authors found models fail to follow DA's tag syntax within thinking traces — a real, current limitation rather than a hypothetical one, and one that matters because thinking-mode reasoning is precisely the regime frontier models increasingly use for the hardest long-context and long-horizon tasks; the paper's "vanilla" baseline is therefore also evaluated without thinking, so the comparison describes a specific (increasingly non-default) operating mode rather than the strongest currently achievable baseline. Similarly, the efficiency headline (0.71x/0.77x decode wall-clock) is a roofline projection under stated utilization assumptions on one hardware target (B200) for the two largest evaluated models, explicitly not a measured benchmark of the described vLLM integration, even though that integration exists and is described in implementation detail (`narrow-evaluation`). Second, no confidence interval, variance estimate, or significance test accompanies any of the headline accuracy or attended-token numbers, despite some individual per-task differences being small (e.g., several RULER sources show accuracy differences of 1-3 points across 128-example draws); the paper's overall pattern is consistent and the mechanism is independently supported by the ablations, but the exact per-task and per-model magnitudes should be read with the understanding that no statistical uncertainty is quantified anywhere in the reported results (`missing-uncertainty`).

What remains well supported after these caveats: the central causal claim — that the attention mask itself, not the changed prompt format, produces both DA's efficiency gain and most of its accuracy cost — is directly demonstrated by the DA-no-mask ablation rather than inferred, and the finding that DA's remaining gap to vanilla shrinks systematically as backbone capability grows, tracked to a specific, independently measured cause (protocol adherence), is a well-supported, mechanistically grounded result rather than an unexplained scaling curve.
