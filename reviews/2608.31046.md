Citation: Yi Ding and Ruqi Zhang. Does On-Policy Distillation Really Distill? From Noisy Teacher to Self-Improvement. arXiv preprint, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31046v1

## What this paper is about

On-Policy Distillation (OPD) trains a student LLM by having a teacher model score the student's own generated tokens via reverse-KL, giving dense token-level supervision instead of RLVR's sparse per-response reward. But the teacher is scoring trajectories it would never have generated itself — they're off-policy for the teacher — so its judgments may not be trustworthy. This paper measures exactly how untrustworthy they are (very), finds the student barely notices, and traces OPD's real gains to something that needs no teacher at all: pushing down the small set of low-probability tokens the student happened to sample. That diagnosis leads directly to a teacher-free, reward-free, hint-free method (On-Policy Self-Adaptation, OPSA) that outperforms OPD itself.

## Extended summary

**Teacher supervision is noisy, and gets noisier with scale.** Using Qwen3-1.7B as student and Qwen3-4B/30B-A3B/235B-A22B-Instruct as teachers of increasing size, the authors define a token's supervision as "noisy" when the teacher's advantage sign disagrees with ground-truth correctness on the final boxed answer token (negative advantage on a correct answer, or positive on an incorrect one). Noise rates rise from 30.6% (4B teacher) to 34.7% (30B-A3B) to 50.6% (235B-A22B); the largest teacher assigns negative advantages to answer tokens almost regardless of correctness (97.8% of correct, 96.6% of incorrect answers get negative advantages) — evidence, the authors argue, that as the teacher gets stronger, student-sampled trajectories become increasingly off-policy from its perspective, making its judgments less discriminating rather than more.

**Students don't care.** A controlled filtering experiment trains OPD on only-noisy, only-clean, or all trajectories; all three converge to comparable AIME24 performance after similar numbers of steps. If the student improves just as much from provably wrong supervision as from correct supervision, the improvement can't be coming from the teacher actually being right.

**Where the gains really come from.** Gradient analysis shows learning vanishes for tokens where the advantage is near zero or where the student's own probability for the sampled token is already near one; empirically, 51.7% of tokens have |advantage| < 1e-4, and these near-zero-advantage tokens are concentrated exactly where the student already assigns high probability (unsurprising, since a confident student and a teacher conditioned on the same prefix tend to agree there). Training only on the student's high-logp tokens — with either the real OPD advantages or random ones — produces no improvement at all. Training instead on the lowest-logp 20% of tokens, using OPD's real advantages, matches full-token OPD. And replacing every advantage on those low-logp tokens with a single fixed negative value (-0.5) also matches OPD's improvement curve — a fixed positive value instead causes outright policy collapse (response length collapsing toward zero, gradient norm exploding, garbled output).

**Entropy determines how much negative signal to apply.** Not all low-probability tokens are alike: a token can be low-probability because the whole distribution is spread out (high entropy) or because the distribution is sharply peaked elsewhere and the token fell in the tail (low entropy). Scaling the fixed negative advantage's magnitude up with a token's relative entropy (among the lowest-20%-logp tokens in a response) outperforms the flat fixed-advantage version and standard OPD itself (50.0% vs. 35.13% Avg@4 on AIME24 for Qwen3-1.7B); scaling it down with entropy instead is unstable and slightly worse than the flat version.

**OPSA.** The resulting method, On-Policy Self-Adaptation, updates only the lowest-20%-logp tokens with a negative advantage whose magnitude scales positively with the token's relative entropy — no teacher, no verifiable reward, no reference-answer hint, at negligible compute overhead versus a forward pass through the policy itself. Mechanistically (Section 4.3, illustrated with concrete token-probability examples), at high-entropy "fork" positions OPSA suppresses sampled tail tokens and redistributes their mass roughly evenly among competing head tokens (preserving diversity rather than collapsing to one head token), while at low-entropy positions it mostly leaves already-confident predictions alone (since those tokens are rarely in the bottom 20% by log-probability).

**Results.** On Qwen3-1.7B, OPSA raises AIME24 Avg@32 from 13.44 to 48.85 (+263% relative), with comparable relative jumps on AIME25 (+264%) and HMMT25 (+307%), and roughly doubles Pass@32 on every in-domain math benchmark; gains persist but shrink at larger/stronger base models (Qwen3-4B: +166% relative on AIME24; Qwen3.5-9B, already at 76.35 Avg@32: +15.0% relative). Out-of-domain transfer to MBPP+ (code) and GPQA-Diamond (general Q&A) is positive but far more modest (+1.2 to +4.5 absolute points, 2-16% relative). Against baselines under matched conditions (Qwen3-1.7B, DAPO-17k, no ground-truth labels used for OPSA/OPSD/TTRL), OPSA beats GRPO, TTRL, OPD, and OPSD on both Avg@32 and Pass@32, averaging 11.04 points higher Avg@32 than the best baseline; TTRL in particular is shown to sharpen the policy toward a local optimum and substantially degrade Pass@k, a failure mode OPSA does not exhibit. A fork-token-masking ablation (excluding high-entropy positions whose head tokens include reflective words like "wait"/"but" from training) largely eliminates OPSA's response-length and accuracy gains, directly supporting the claim that its benefit comes from redistributing probability at these specific reasoning-fork positions rather than from token suppression in general. A separate diversity analysis (pairwise Jaccard distance over 32 sampled responses per problem) shows OPSA does not collapse long-form response diversity relative to the base model, consistent with its Pass@32 gains.

**Self-stated limitations.** Experiments are capped at 9B parameters due to compute, so scaling to larger or mixture-of-experts models is untested; the authors explicitly flag that OPSA "primarily operates by redistributing the policy's existing probability mass," so its benefit may shrink for heavily post-trained models whose distributions are already sharp and low-entropy (consistent with the diminishing relative gains observed from 1.7B to 9B in their own results) and that it likely does not expand the policy's underlying exploration frontier, reflected in more modest gains when thinking mode (already a stronger exploration regime) is enabled.

## Learnings

The controlled-filtering experiment (training exclusively on trajectories the paper's own noise definition marks as misleading, and finding equivalent improvement to clean-only or full training) is a clean, directly falsifiable way to test whether a distillation method's gains actually come from the teacher's judgment being correct — a reusable diagnostic for any setup where a scoring or supervision signal is suspected of being systematically unreliable but the trained model improves anyway.

The finding that a single fixed negative advantage on low-probability tokens reproduces most of a sophisticated teacher-based method's gains, while a fixed positive advantage on the same tokens causes catastrophic collapse, is a specific, mechanistic instance of negative-sample reinforcement's known effectiveness (cited from concurrent work) — worth generalizing as a design heuristic: before crediting an elaborate supervision signal with an improvement, check whether the improvement's sign structure (which tokens get pushed down vs. up, and by how much) survives replacing the signal with the simplest version that has the same sign pattern.

The entropy-conditioned redistribution mechanism — suppress tail tokens harder at high-entropy "fork" positions while leaving low-entropy, already-confident predictions alone — is a concrete instantiation of a broader, transferable design principle for self-improvement without external supervision: use the model's own uncertainty structure to decide where correction is safe to apply aggressively (uncertain, high-branching decision points) versus where it should be left untouched (confident, likely-correct predictions), rather than applying a uniform correction strength everywhere.

## Verification

Central and multi-layered: the noise-rate measurement, the noisy-vs-clean filtering experiment, the token-selection ablations (top-logp vs. low-logp, with real vs. random advantages), the fixed-advantage sign experiments (negative vs. positive), the entropy-scaling direction comparison (δ=1 vs. δ=-1 vs. δ=0), and the fork-token-masking ablation each isolate one specific mechanistic claim rather than reporting only the final method's aggregate benchmark score. The diversity analysis (Jaccard distance) is a targeted check against a specific, named alternative failure mode (diversity collapse from suppressing low-probability tokens) rather than an assumed non-issue. What is not independently verified: the paper's own noisy-supervision definition is restricted to the final verifiable answer token because intermediate reasoning-step correctness has no available ground truth, a limitation the authors state explicitly rather than papering over; and no multi-seed or training-run-to-run variance is reported for the headline Avg@32/Pass@32 numbers (each is itself an average over 32 sampled rollouts per problem, but the underlying training run appears to be single-seed), so how much of the large reported gains would replicate under different training seeds is not established.

## Field context

The paper positions itself directly against two lines of on-policy training work it argues share the same unresolved vulnerability: On-Policy Distillation (OPD, building on GKD and MiniLLM, most recently formalized with the K1 estimator) and On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), which replaces the external teacher with the policy itself conditioned on hints but keeps the same score-the-student's-trajectory paradigm and thus, the authors argue, the same unresolved off-policy-supervision reliability question. It also distinguishes itself from label-free policy-improvement methods (self-rewarding LLM-as-judge, TTRL/EMPO's majority-vote pseudo-labels, Intuitor's trajectory-level self-certainty reward), arguing these depend on the policy's initial capability and risk reinforcing an incorrect mode when the correct answer isn't already near a distribution peak, whereas OPSA's token-level entropy-adaptive signal does not force convergence toward any single self-generated label. No paper currently in this corpus addresses on-policy distillation, token-level advantage assignment, or entropy-adaptive RL post-training specifically, so this is a novel connection point within the corpus rather than one that corroborates or conflicts with an existing review.

## Critical discussion

The paper's diagnostic chain (Sections 2-3) is unusually rigorous for a paper that ultimately proposes a new method: each mechanistic claim (noise is real and scale-dependent; noise doesn't matter; high-logp tokens don't matter; a fixed negative advantage matches OPD; entropy sets the right magnitude) is tested by a dedicated controlled experiment rather than inferred from the final method's performance alone, which makes the resulting explanation for why OPSA works considerably more credible than a purely outcome-driven ablation study would.

Two things temper how broadly the headline numbers should be read. First, the striking relative-gain percentages emphasized throughout (263%, 264%, 307%) are all in-domain math-competition results on a base (non-thinking-mode) 1.7B model; the paper's own out-of-domain results (MBPP+, GPQA-Diamond) and its own larger/stronger-model results (Qwen3.5-9B) show a clear, self-reported shrinking pattern — OOD relative gains are 2-16% rather than 100%+, and the 9B model's relative AIME24 gain (15.0%) is roughly 17x smaller than the 1.7B model's (263.5%). The authors explain this pattern mechanistically (OPSA redistributes existing probability mass and cannot help a distribution that's already sharp), which is a genuinely honest and specific limitation statement, but a reader who anchors on the abstract's headline percentage without reading the OOD and larger-model rows in Table 2 could substantially overestimate how broadly the effect generalizes (`narrow-evaluation`). Second, all experiments are capped at 9B parameters (explicitly self-acknowledged), and no training-seed variance is reported for the main results, so the exact magnitude of the reported gains — as opposed to their direction and the underlying mechanism, which are well supported by the ablation chain — has not been shown to be stable across independent training runs (`missing-uncertainty`).

What remains well supported after these caveats: the core mechanistic claim — that OPD's gains in this setting come substantially from suppressing low-probability student-sampled tokens rather than from genuine teacher-knowledge transfer — is backed by a coherent, multi-step chain of controlled experiments rather than a single correlational observation, and the resulting teacher-free method's benefit is causally supported by the fork-token-masking ablation rather than asserted from aggregate scores alone. The claim that this specific technique meaningfully improves already-strong or much-larger models, or that it substitutes for genuine external supervision in domains without DAPO-17k-like training distributions, is not established to the same standard and the authors are explicit that this remains open.
