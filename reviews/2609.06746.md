## What this paper is about

Several recent vision-language models claim to "reason" through a latent, non-verbal internal state that carries visual information across steps. This paper points out a basic gap in that claim: a latent state can contain useful visual information without the model's final answer actually depending on it, especially when the model's ordinary multimodal context is still sitting there as an easier shortcut. The authors show this is true of several published latent visual reasoners, then build a method, CVRR, that architecturally removes the shortcut — forcing the answer to pass only through the recurrent latent state — while trying not to lose the visual competence the pretrained model already has.

## Extended summary

**Motivating diagnostics.** Two preliminary experiments motivate the design. First (Figure 1a), replacing several existing latent visual reasoners' latent states with a blank-image latent, a mismatched latent from a different example, or norm-matched noise — while leaving the model's normal multimodal answer context untouched — barely changes accuracy for some methods (UniVLR moves by at most 0.5 points across all three corruptions), showing the model isn't actually relying on that latent content when an easier path remains available. Second (Figure 1b), on the frozen pretrained Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, the question-token hidden state taken from a normal multimodal forward pass (75.4% on V*) already carries almost all the visual competence of the full multimodal state (75.9%), while a text-only question representation, even with raw visual features concatenated back on, recovers only 35.6% — meaning the pretrained model has already folded most of its usable visual competence into the question tokens themselves by some intermediate layer, and a latent reasoner that starts from scratch is discarding this for free.

**Method (CVRR).** Using layer-wise activation patching with contrastive image pairs sharing a question, the authors locate a "causal visual-read boundary" (layer 20 of Qwen2.5-VL-7B) past which visual information's downstream causal influence starts declining. CVRR initializes its recurrent state from the question-token hidden state at that boundary (carrying pretrained visual competence), then repeatedly updates this state for T steps using one shared, LoRA-adapted decoder layer that re-reads the same fixed visual-token rows at every step (a looped/recurrent-transformer design, following Universal Transformers and related recurrent-depth work, but applied here to force repeated re-reading of persistent visual evidence rather than for depth-efficiency per se). Before decoding, all visual rows and the original multimodal KV cache are discarded; only the final recurrent state and a separately-built image-free text-only KV cache reach the frozen upper decoder, so the recurrent state is the only route by which image information can reach the answer. Training only updates the shared LoRA parameters via plain answer-token cross-entropy, with no auxiliary latent-supervision or reconstruction loss — path necessity is enforced by the architecture, not incentivized by the loss.

**Main results.** Trained on Visual CoT (438K QA pairs with bounding boxes) and evaluated on V* (191 examples), MMVP (300 questions, 150 pairs), BLINK (5 subsets + overall), and MME-RealWorld-Lite, CVRR reaches 81.2% on V*, 52.7% MMVP pair accuracy, and 55.2% BLINK overall under its strict no-bypass interface. The key comparison is against seven existing latent visual reasoners (LVR, Monet, SkiLa, Laser, UniVLR, HyLaR, PEARL) retrained from scratch under the identical strict interface (same data, same no-bypass decoder, same training budget) rather than compared only in their original, bypass-permitting form: every one of them collapses under this constraint, reaching at most 39.8% on V*, 2.7% MMVP pair accuracy, and 38.8% BLINK overall — a roughly 40-50 point drop from their own bypass-permitting numbers. A full-multimodal SFT control (same Visual CoT training data, but keeping the ordinary multimodal answer path open) confirms the gap isn't just a training-data effect: CVRR matches or exceeds this control on V*, MMVP, and BLINK despite removing the standard answer path entirely, though it drops slightly more than the SFT control on MME-RealWorld-Lite (-4.1 vs -6.1 points from backbone, so CVRR is actually not worse there either).

**Component analysis and causal verification.** An ablation (Table 2) shows all three pieces matter: replacing the native multimodal initialization with a text-only anchor collapses performance to the no-bypass-baseline range (37.2% V*, 2.0% MMVP pair) even with visual re-reading and a retrained transition intact; removing visual re-reading (keeping native init, T=4) costs 12.0 V* points and 34.7 MMVP-pair points; removing the learned transition (keeping native init and re-reading) costs 4.7 and 20.0 points. A battery of causal interventions (Figures 3-5) supports that the mechanism is doing what it claims: corrupting the final recurrent state degrades accuracy sharply (81.2% to 14.0% under a matched-state swap) but restoring the (removed) multimodal prefix partially recovers it, showing the informative alternative route the strict interface is designed to close off; crossed question/visual-evidence states show clean visual evidence pulling a wrong initial state toward the correct answer and vice versa, an effect that nearly disappears when question-to-visual attention connections are blocked; a same-question MMVP intervention isolates image-conditioned content specifically (not just general state disruption) as the source of the sensitivity; and blocking visual attention region-by-region across recurrent steps shows the causally influential image regions shift from step to step rather than staying fixed.

## Learnings

The paper's central methodological point generalizes well beyond visual reasoning: showing that a proposed internal representation is informative (predictive of the right answer when probed, or correlated with task-relevant content) is not the same evidence as showing the model's own inference actually routes through it, and the right test for the latter is an intervention performed while every other route to the answer stays open — corrupting the latent state while leaving the ordinary bypass available, and checking whether accuracy actually moves. Several published latent-reasoning claims in this exact subfield fail this specific test (Figure 1a), which is a useful, transferable red flag for evaluating any "the model reasons through latent state X" claim elsewhere.

The preliminary finding that a pretrained VLM's own question-token representations, taken from a normal forward pass at an intermediate layer, already carry nearly all of the visual competence otherwise attributed to the full multimodal context (75.4% vs 75.9% on V*) — while raw visual features bolted onto a text-only representation do not reconstruct this (35.6%) — is a specific, useful characterization of where visual grounding actually lives inside a pretrained VLM, independent of whether one adopts CVRR's particular recurrent architecture.

Enforcing a scientific claim ("prediction depends on mechanism X") by construction (architecturally removing every alternative path) rather than by post-hoc discovery or a training incentive is a clean general strategy when a loss function alone cannot be trusted to induce the property being claimed — here, ordinary cross-entropy training with a bypass available produced models where the proposed latent mechanism was, per Figure 1a, largely inert.

## Verification

Verification is the paper's main contribution, not a side note. The core claim (that CVRR's recurrent state is causally necessary and causally visually-grounded, not merely informative) is checked with a chain of five distinct causal-intervention experiments (matched/blank/noise state replacement with and without bypass restoration; crossed question/visual-evidence swaps with attention blocking; same-question MMVP swaps isolating image content from question content; and region-blocking across recurrent steps), each targeting a different alternative explanation (an intact-but-generic state working just as well; question disruption rather than visual-content disruption; a static rather than actively-used visual signal) rather than reporting one aggregate ablation number and declaring the mechanism verified. The activation-patching procedure used to locate the recurrent boundary (§3.1) is itself a controlled, contrastive-pair causal method rather than a heuristic layer choice. What is not present: no confidence intervals, variance across seeds, or significance tests accompany any of the headline accuracy numbers or point-difference claims (Table 1, Table 2, Figures 3-5), and the seven retrained no-bypass baselines are each reported as a single run.

## Field context

This paper sits inside, and directly stress-tests, the recent latent (non-verbal) reasoning literature it reviews in its Introduction and Appendix A.1 — continuous latent reasoning for text (Hao et al., Coconut) extended to vision through latent visual tokens or recurrent hidden-state updates (the seven baselines it retrains: LVR, Monet, SkiLa, Laser, UniVLR, HyLaR, PEARL). Its architectural mechanism — a single shared decoder layer reused across recurrent steps via LoRA adaptation — places it in the looped/recurrent-depth transformer family (Universal Transformers; recurrent-depth transformers), the same architectural lineage as "[LoopFormer](2602.11451.md)" (reviewed this batch), though the two papers pursue different goals with that shared mechanism: LoopFormer asks whether a looped model trained at one loop count can generalize smoothly to a different, user-chosen loop count at inference, while this paper uses the loop purely as a vehicle for repeated visual re-reading and never varies loop count at inference. The paper's central diagnostic — that latent informativeness does not imply causal necessity — is explicitly framed by the authors as a critique applicable across the latent-visual-reasoning subfield rather than a claim specific to any one prior method, and no paper currently in this corpus previously addressed causal necessity testing for latent visual/multimodal reasoning, making this a new critical-methodology entry point for the corpus.

## Critical discussion

The paper's central empirical contribution — that several published latent visual reasoners fail a strict causal-necessity test while a purpose-built architecture (CVRR) can pass it without sacrificing much accuracy — is unusually well supported by the intervention design itself: retraining every baseline under the identical no-bypass interface, rather than comparing CVRR's no-bypass numbers against baselines' original bypass-permitting numbers, is exactly the controlled comparison this claim requires, and the authors explicitly note this distinction (§4.2) rather than eliding it.

Two things temper how far the numbers should be read. First, all four evaluation benchmarks are small (V* has 191 examples, MMVP 300 questions over 150 pairs, MME-RealWorld-Lite is explicitly a "Lite" subset), and no confidence interval, variance, or repeated-run result accompanies any reported accuracy or point-difference in Table 1, Table 2, or the causal-intervention figures; a number like the 52.7% vs 2.7% MMVP-pair gap between CVRR and the best no-bypass baseline is dramatic, but with n=150 pairs and a single run per condition, its precise margin should be read as this specific evaluation's outcome rather than a tightly bounded effect size (`narrow-evaluation`, `missing-uncertainty`). Second, the causal-intervention experiments in §5 (Figures 3-5) are themselves run on unspecified, apparently modest sample sizes (the MME-RealWorld-Lite region-blocking analysis and the same-question MMVP intervention do not state their exact n in the extracted main text), so while the qualitative pattern across interventions is coherent and mutually reinforcing (multiple independent tests point the same direction), the exact percentages attached to each intervention carry the same lack of uncertainty quantification as the main results.

The authors' own stated limitations are honest and specific rather than boilerplate: the causal visual-read boundary is backbone-specific (located anew via activation patching for Qwen2.5-VL-7B, not shown to transfer to other VLMs), the mechanistic causal analyses are conducted mainly in multiple-choice-style settings rather than open-ended generation, and the design commits to a fixed, persistent visual-evidence representation rather than one that could adaptively re-attend to different image regions or resolutions during recurrence — a genuine scope limitation the paper flags rather than one this review must surface independently.

What remains well supported after these caveats: the qualitative and mechanistic core of the claim — that latent informativeness and causal necessity are separable properties, that several published latent visual reasoners exhibit the former without the latter under a controlled test, and that CVRR's specific design (native multimodal initialization plus persistent visual re-reading plus a fully closed bypass) achieves both properties simultaneously on the benchmarks tested — is supported by a genuinely multi-angle body of intervention evidence, even where the precise numerical margins carry more uncertainty than the tables alone convey.
