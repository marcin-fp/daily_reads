Citation: Wei, Z., Pang, L., Liu, J., Shi, W., Deng, J., Xu, S., Duan, Z., Sun, F., Shen, H., Cheng, X. The Evolution of Thought: Tracking LLM Overthinking via Reasoning Dynamics Analysis. arXiv:2508.17627, 2025.

## What this paper is about

Modern reasoning LLMs generate an explicit "thinking" trace before answering, and longer thinking generally helps, up to a point: past that point, models keep generating reasoning tokens without the answer changing or improving, a widely-noted phenomenon called overthinking. This paper asks a more mechanistic question than prior work: what is actually happening, moment to moment, inside a reasoning trace as it transitions from useful computation to redundant computation? By tracking both the length and the internal semantic content of a model's reasoning as it unfolds, the authors identify a specific transition point, which they call the Reasoning Completion Point (RCP), after which further thinking tokens no longer change anything that matters, and they build a lightweight method to detect this point during decoding and stop early, cutting token usage substantially without hurting accuracy.

## Extended summary

The paper analyzes reasoning dynamics via a stepwise truncation protocol: for a given prompt, the thinking trajectory is segmented into sentence-level steps (via NLTK), and at every possible truncation point the model is forced to stop thinking (by injecting the end-of-thinking delimiter, e.g. </think>) and immediately produce its final content/answer, allowing the researchers to observe how content length and correctness change as a function of how much thinking was allowed. This reveals two coupled patterns. First, Reasoning Length Dynamics show "thinking-content compensation": early on, when thinking is cut short, the model compensates by generating longer content to work out the answer there instead, and truncated-early runs that fail tend to produce noticeably shorter content than truncated-early runs that succeed; once thinking has run long enough, content length collapses to a stable, concise band and stays there even as thinking keeps growing for another 100+ steps, meaning extra thinking beyond that point manifests purely as redundant computation rather than any change in the eventual answer. Second, Reasoning Semantic Dynamics use a complementary lens: by resampling multiple independent content continuations at each truncation step, embedding them (via the Qwen3 Embedding model), and tracking their trajectory in a PCA-projected latent space, the authors observe an initial phase of large, volatile semantic jumps (active exploration of candidate solutions) followed by a transition into a stable neighborhood with only small, repetitive oscillations (a quantified "semantic path convergence," measured via a KL-divergence-based convergence residual against an estimated terminal distribution, and separately validated by fitting a 95% confidence ellipse around post-transition points, which is found to contain 97.3%, 94.3%, and 96.5% of subsequent points across three worked examples).

These two independently-motivated signals, length-based stabilization and semantic convergence, are combined into a single formal definition of the RCP: the earliest truncation step at which both content length has stabilized (below a small threshold) and semantic convergence residual has dropped to its low plateau. Because this boundary is instance-specific and depends on quantities (full trajectory, resampled continuations) unavailable during actual decoding, the paper distills it into an online-usable signal: labeling instances offline with their gold RCP step, the authors observe that RCP consistently coincides with the first emergence of the eventual final answer in the trace, and that this moment is reliably marked by a sharp drop in the rank of the thinking-termination token (</think>) in the model's own next-token distribution, a signal that is intrinsic to the model, requires no extra computation, and is invariant to how the final answer is phrased (unlike trying to detect the answer's literal surface form). A decision tree fit on Math500 using this rank signal (plus short-horizon rank history) is distilled into three deterministic stopping rules (immediate saturation when rank ≤ 5; a progressive-convergence pattern of monotonically improving rank over a window; and a sustained-plateau condition combining short-term stability and longer-term persistence in a low-rank region), forming the Reasoning Completion Point Detector (RCPD), applied online by forcing termination once triggered.

Evaluated on AIME24, AIME25, and GPQA-Diamond using Qwen3 (8B/14B/30B-A3B/32B) and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-8B, RCPD reduces average token usage by up to 44% relative to full decoding while keeping accuracy within a small margin of full decoding (and in several cases matching it exactly, e.g. Qwen3-8B on AIME24: 72.22% accuracy at both full decoding and RCPD, using 56% fewer tokens on average across benchmarks). Against a Budget-Force baseline given an explicit, retrospective, iso-computational advantage (its fixed token budget is tuned per model/benchmark to match RCPD's average token usage after the fact, an advantage unavailable at real deployment time), RCPD still clearly outperforms it, since a single fixed budget either wastes tokens on easy instances or truncates hard instances before they reach convergence, whereas RCPD adapts per instance. Against DEER, an existing training-free early-exit method based on reflective cue words and answer-confidence thresholds, RCPD achieves comparable or better accuracy at substantially lower token cost across most settings. Against S-GRPO, a training-based early-exit method requiring additional reinforcement learning on the LLM itself (reported using numbers taken directly from its original paper, not reproduced under this paper's own evaluation setup), RCPD is competitive despite requiring no additional training, outperforming it on GPQA-D while underperforming on AIME24.

A targeted qualitative and causal follow-up analysis (Appendix F) examines what actually happens during Post-RCP "redundant" generation: on one specific AIME problem, the authors manually edit an intermediate tentative answer in the reasoning trace (swapping a stated guess of 12 for 16, and vice versa) and rerun 64 independent completions from that edited point. In both directions, roughly 49-51 of 64 trials adopt the injected (artificially altered) intermediate answer as the final answer rather than recovering the model's own originally-correct conclusion, which the authors interpret as evidence that Post-RCP generation functions mainly as rationalization of whatever conclusion has already been reached ("cognitive inertia") rather than genuine, independent re-verification, consistent with the qualitative examples given elsewhere in the appendix of models repeatedly re-deriving the same speculative or already-stated answer through superficially different phrasings.

## Learnings

The paper's central, broadly transferable finding is a specific mechanistic account of what overthinking actually consists of at the token level: it is not merely "more tokens without better answers" in the abstract, but a concrete, two-part signature, content length flattening out combined with the model's internal semantic representation settling into a stable neighborhood, that together mark the point past which additional computation stops mattering. This gives future researchers or practitioners a concrete, checkable definition to test for in other reasoning-model settings, rather than relying only on an aggregate length-versus-accuracy correlation.

A second, more directly actionable learning is the specific detection signal the paper identifies: the rank of the thinking-termination delimiter token in the model's own next-token distribution drops sharply exactly when the final answer first emerges internally, and this signal requires no additional model calls, fine-tuning, or answer-format parsing, since it is read directly off standard decoding. This is a concretely reusable, low-overhead technique for anyone building an early-exit or adaptive-compute system for delimiter-based reasoning models, distinct from and apparently more robust than either a fixed token budget or reflective-cue-word heuristics (per the paper's own head-to-head comparisons).

A third, more cautionary learning comes from the confirmation-bias experiment: extended post-convergence reasoning in these models does not function as genuine self-correction or independent re-derivation, since injecting a false intermediate conclusion causes the model to overwhelmingly rationalize and adopt that false conclusion rather than catch and correct it, even though the model had originally reached the correct answer unprompted. This is a specific, causally-demonstrated (via direct intervention on the reasoning trace, not just correlational observation) counter-example to the assumption that "letting a model think longer" provides a meaningful self-checking function once it has already converged on a conclusion.

## Verification

The paper grounds each of its two core dynamics claims in a specific, described measurement procedure rather than only impressionistic description: the length-based compensation and redundancy patterns are measured via the stepwise truncation intervention itself (an actual causal manipulation, not passive observation, since the model is forced to stop thinking and continue at every candidate step), and the semantic convergence claim is operationalized quantitatively via a KL-divergence-based convergence residual against an estimated terminal distribution, with a separate, testable stability check (the 95% confidence-ellipse in-basin fraction, reported as 97.3%/94.3%/96.5% across three examples) offered specifically to make the "stable neighborhood" claim falsifiable rather than only visually asserted from a 2D PCA plot. The RCPD detector itself is evaluated against multiple realistic baselines, including one (Budget Force) deliberately strengthened with an oracle-level, otherwise-unavailable advantage specifically to avoid an easy strawman comparison, and a controlled ablation (Table 2, comparing RCPD's temporal-pattern rules against a simpler single-threshold rank rule) that isolates which specific component of the detector (short-horizon temporal pattern versus a single instantaneous rank check) contributes to its robustness. The confirmation-bias claim in Appendix F is verified via a genuine causal intervention (directly editing the reasoning trace and rerunning with 64 independent samples per direction) rather than only observational correlation, and the result is checked for symmetry (both a 12-to-16 and a 16-to-12 injection direction) to rule out a direction-specific artifact.

Verification is comparatively thinner in a few places. The S-GRPO comparison, one of only two baselines representing an alternative overthinking-mitigation paradigm (training-based early exit), uses numbers reported directly from that paper's own results rather than a reproduction under this paper's own decoding configuration, prompt format, and hardware, an acknowledged substitution (the paper is transparent that these are "results directly from their paper") that nonetheless means the head-to-head comparison is not a strictly controlled, apples-to-apples evaluation, and some cells for this baseline are simply missing ("-", not reported for AIME25). The confirmation-bias/cognitive-inertia claim, while causally demonstrated, is demonstrated on a single AIME problem (64 resampled completions from one edited trace in each direction), so while the specific numerical result (49/64, 51/64) is a real, non-trivial effect size, generalizing the broader interpretive claim that "Post-RCP generation seldom performs genuine re-verification" across the wide range of problem types and models studied elsewhere in the paper rests on this one worked case plus a small set of additional qualitative examples, rather than a systematic, multi-instance version of the same causal-injection experiment.

## Field context

The paper explicitly situates itself within a fast-growing "efficient reasoning" literature it organizes into three categories following prior surveys (Sui et al. 2025, Wang et al. 2025): post-training-based methods that require retraining with length-aware objectives or rewards, prompt-based methods that instruct models to reason more concisely, and early-exit methods that intervene during decoding itself; RCPD is positioned in the last category, specifically within the newer, training-free sub-line typified by DEER, distinguishing itself from methods requiring auxiliary classifiers, bandit controllers, difficulty estimators, or (in S-GRPO's case) additional reinforcement-learning training of the base model. Its core empirical motivation, that reasoning-length scaling is non-monotonic and overthinking causes diminishing or negative returns, builds directly on recently-cited empirical observations (Ghosal et al. 2025's "mirage of test-time scaling," Su et al. 2025, Wu et al. 2025) rather than introducing that observation itself; its distinct contribution is the mechanistic, dynamics-level explanation (thinking-content compensation plus semantic path convergence) for why this non-monotonicity occurs, rather than simply documenting that it occurs. Within this corpus, the Appendix F confirmation-bias finding, that extended post-convergence reasoning tends to rationalize rather than genuinely re-examine an already-reached conclusion, is directly relevant to work on LLM self-correction reviewed elsewhere (e.g. [24selfcor]), since it offers a concrete, causally-demonstrated mechanism for why naively prompting or allowing a model to "think more" or "double check its work" often fails to catch errors: once the model's internal state has converged (in this paper's specific, measured sense), continued generation is not functioning as independent verification at all, a mechanistic complement to behavioral findings elsewhere in the corpus that LLM self-correction without external feedback is unreliable.

## Critical discussion

The paper's central strength is combining two independently-motivated signals, surface-level length dynamics and latent-space semantic dynamics, into a single, jointly-defined, testable construct (the RCP) rather than relying on either alone, and then validating that construct's practical usefulness with a real early-exit system evaluated against multiple baselines, including one deliberately strengthened to avoid an easy win. The confirmation-bias experiment is a genuine methodological high point: it is a direct causal intervention rather than a passive correlation, with a symmetric check ruling out a simple directional artifact, and it materially strengthens the paper's mechanistic story (why post-RCP reasoning is redundant, not merely that it correlates with no accuracy change).

The most significant limitation is the S-GRPO comparison's reliance on externally-reported numbers rather than a matched reproduction, which is disclosed but does weaken the specific comparative claim ("RCPD... rivals the training-based S-GRPO baseline") since decoding configuration, prompt template, and hardware differences between the two papers' setups are not controlled for, and one benchmark (AIME25) has no S-GRPO number to compare against at all; this is a real limitation on a competitive claim against the paper's only training-based comparator, though it does not affect the paper's comparisons against Full, No-Think, BF, and DEER, all of which appear to be run under this paper's own controlled setup. A second limitation is the narrow evidentiary base underlying the paper's most striking mechanistic claim (confirmation bias / cognitive inertia during Post-RCP generation): the causal injection experiment is conducted on a single problem instance, and while 64 resampled completions per direction is a reasonably large sample for that one instance, the paper does not report whether this specific injection experiment was replicated across additional problems to check whether the roughly 75-80% adoption rate of the injected answer generalizes in magnitude, or whether it is specific to this particular problem's reasoning structure; the broader qualitative examples in Appendix F (repeated verification, repeated speculative derivation) support the general overthinking-characterization claim but are illustrative rather than a systematic multi-instance version of the causal test. Finally, per the paper's own stated Limitations, all reasoning-dynamics analysis and RCPD evaluation are restricted to the Qwen3 and DeepSeek-R1 model families specifically because these expose explicit thinking delimiters needed for the intervention; whether the same RCP phase-transition structure holds for closed-source reasoning APIs, tool-augmented agents, or multimodal reasoning models is explicitly left open by the authors rather than claimed.

What remains well supported: the thinking-content compensation and semantic path convergence patterns are demonstrated via a genuine causal intervention (stepwise forced truncation) rather than passive observation, consistently across the two model families tested; the RCPD early-exit method reliably reduces token usage (up to 44%) while preserving accuracy relative to full decoding and clearly outperforms both a naive fixed-budget baseline and an existing training-free early-exit method under matched evaluation conditions; and the specific causal demonstration that an artificially injected intermediate conclusion is overwhelmingly adopted rather than corrected during extended reasoning is a real, direct result for the one instance tested, strongly suggestive of, though not yet established as fully general evidence for, the paper's broader claim about the nature of Post-RCP computation.
